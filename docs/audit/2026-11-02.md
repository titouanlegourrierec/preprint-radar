- **Scope.** Six axes, chosen with the maintainer for a product that is now built (v1.1.0,
  public, a stable data format): the maintainability of the extraction (EXT), the real
  quality of the affiliations (AQ), the reliability observed over a month of runs (OPS),
  growth (SCALE), the public repository (PUB), and the site on today's data (WEB).
- **Method.**
  - One independent reviewer per axis, read-only, with the first audit and the roadmap in
    hand: what the first audit's items did, dropped or deferred, and the decisions taken,
    are not reported again unless they regressed.
  - Claims are measured wherever possible: every regex's alternatives replayed and knocked
    out one by one against the tests and the 431 cached pages (EXT); 60 stored papers
    checked by hand against their pages, seed 20261102 (AQ); the logs of all 60 data runs
    since 2026-10-01 (OPS); git packs, a local build and projections to September 2027
    (SCALE); a fresh clone with no keys, the workflows and the public logs (PUB); a
    production build driven by Playwright with 4x CPU throttling and 4G and 3G profiles,
    and the real search code in Node (WEB).
  - Every finding rated P0 or P1 was then put to an adversarial verifier, asked whether it
    reproduces, whether the severity is right for a one-maintainer static site, and whether
    it is already handled. All six P1 findings reproduced; all six were lowered to P2.
  - No request reached arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex or ROR; GitHub only through `gh`,
    read-only, on this repository.
- **Labels and priorities** are the first audit's: **Verified** (in code, `file:line`, or
  measured), **Hypothesis** (with the check that would confirm it), **Idea**; **P0**
  critical, **P1** high value, **P2** useful, **P3** exploratory; effort **S** (under half
  a day), **M** (a day or two), **L** (a week), **XL**; kind **Quick**, **Medium**,
  **Architectural**.
- **IDs** use new prefixes (EXT, AQ, OPS, SCALE, PUB, WEB), so that they never collide
  with the first audit's. The priority shown is the final one, after verification.
- Real author e-mail addresses quoted by the reviewers are written `author@<domain>` here.

## 1. Executive summary

**No P0 and no P1: 36 P2 and 19 P3 items, 55 in all.** The
first audit found a product to build; this one finds a working system whose debts are
narrower: a safety net thinner than its 100% coverage suggests, a promise the pipeline does
not keep, and costs that grow with the data in a few places.

What matters most, in the order I would take it:

1. **A promise the pipeline does not keep (PUB-1).** The About page says a removal or a
   correction "stays"; nothing keeps either: the weekly archive picks a removed paper again,
   and every extraction bump (two in four days) reads a corrected paper again. REL-11, the
   removal channel, was deferred until someone asks; the page already invites them to.
2. **Cheap fixes with a real downside.** One regex backtracks exponentially, 3.3 s at 22
   mailbox names and four times more every two, on a page that would then stall every run
   that reads the cache again (EXT-1, S). Six real author addresses came back into code,
   tests and the roadmap since CI-13's cleanup, with nothing to stop the next (PUB-4, S).
   The data jobs keep a token that can push to `main` in `.git/config` for hours while
   third-party code runs, and `main` has no ruleset (PUB-2, PUB-3).
3. **The extraction's safety net (EXT-2, EXT-5, AQ-1).** 232 of 435 single-rule knockouts
   leave every test green, 19 of them changing real cached pages; all of the institutions'
   `_ORGANIZATION_RE` can go with 1,434 tests green. The quality report flagged 2 of the 12
   defective papers of the hand sample and reads no archive paper. A gold set (the hand
   sample, kept) and tests on the live rules would make the next versions safer than the
   Extraction diff alone.
4. **The data itself.** On the hand sample, 80% of papers have exactly their page's
   affiliations and mapping, 8% a serious defect (an institution lost, authors misplaced);
   11% of institutions are wrong or unresolved. The fixable classes are narrow: footnote
   digits read as markers (AQ-2), numbered lines cut to their first line (AQ-3), places
   named as institutions (AQ-4), 2,220 strings that could take the ROR id their name has
   elsewhere, 589 papers missing from the map (AQ-6).
5. **The site at four times the data (WEB-1 to WEB-6).** A query in the URL, or typed before
   the background index ends, freezes the page while the whole period is indexed (about
   1 s for a month on a desktop, several on a phone). Highly cited "All years" downloads
   16 MB gzipped to show 10,000 papers; the default week waits for all its day files;
   abstracts are 72% of what the lists download, collapsed by default.
6. **Growth (SCALE-1).** The repository, not the site, hits a limit first: GitHub stores
   the weekly-rewritten archive years as poor deltas (117 MB reported, 30 MB repacked), so
   the canary's 500 MB warning comes between March and August 2027, with one category.
   The site stays near 300 MB once pruning starts; the migration is already in the runbook.

Reliability is good: *Update data* 48 of 50 runs green, *Archive* 9 of 10, every announced
day stored, dispatches on time to the second; the two incidents (2026-10-20, 2026-11-01)
each got a fix (AFF-32, REL-13). What remains are single copies (the page cache in one
Actions cache entry, the archive's pages on the maintainer's machine) and silent paths
(OPS-1 to OPS-6).

## 3. Maintainability of the extraction (EXT)

### Assessment

The extraction works and is fast, but its safety net is thinner than it looks. sources/arxiv_html.py has grown from 694 lines at the first audit to 2,279. It holds 84 module-level regexes and a 100-line version changelog in its docstring (L25-124), and it went through 44 commits since 2026-10-01.

Speed is not a concern. Over the local page cache (430 pages with a stored paper, scratch script EXT/time_cache.py), extraction takes 3.5 s in all: 8.1 ms mean, 6.6 ms median, 52 ms at most, including a 420-author page. That is about 1,800 times less than the 15 s crawl delay. Each page costs 24 BeautifulSoup parses (10,523 for 430 pages), because nodes are copied by re-serializing them. This costs nothing that matters.

What I measured:
- **Regex alternatives reached.** I recorded every input to every module-level regex and replayed it against copies that capture each alternative (regex_spy.py, alts.py). Of 656 alternatives in arxiv_html and institutions (country names and negative lookarounds left out), the unit tests reach 250, the 84 golden fixtures 121, and the cached pages 217.
- **Knockout tests.** I disabled each of the 84 regexes, and separately each of 351 top-level alternatives, then ran the 9 extraction test files (run_mutant.py). 232 of the 435 knockouts left every test green. 19 of those 232 change real cached pages, so live rules exist that no test protects.
- **Institution rules.** Disabling all of `_ORGANIZATION_RE` in institutions.py leaves all 1,434 unit tests green.
- **Evidence pages.** Of the 133 arXiv ids that docs/extraction.md and the module cite as evidence, 41 have neither a fixture nor a test that names them, and 24 of those sit in the local cache.
- **Regex timing.** One regex backtracks exponentially: `_MAILBOX_LIST_RE`, 3.3 s at 22 items and four times longer every 2 items. Two others are quadratic (`_TRAILING_ANCHOR_RE` and `_OBFUSCATED_EMAIL_RE`, about 10 s at 32 KB), but the longest real note in the cache is 983 characters, so they are not a risk.
- **Data defects.** About 35 stored HTML-extracted strings, out of 19,264 in 8,463 papers at extractor 21, carry defects that no rule or quality sign catches: e-mail fragments, TeX math markers, zero-width characters, note sentences.
- **Unicode.** Two gaps: ASCII- and English-only glue rules ("Institut für InformatikUniversität Zürich" stays one string, with 0 such cases among 16,334 stored strings), and `name_key` does not fold ł, ø and đ.
- **venue.py and latex.py.** Instrumented under the unit tests, venue.py's alternatives are reached 68 of 202 times and latex.py's 7 of 14. These are mostly vocabulary tables, so I did not mutate them.

The module splits cleanly. An AST call graph over the proposed parts shows a single cycle (cleaning and line joining) and four mapping-to-sources edges, so a pure move is feasible and the golden fixtures plus the Extraction diff workflow can prove it changed nothing. All scripts and logs are in the audit's scratch scripts (EXT)

### What is good

- Every rule is documented with the arXiv id of the page that motivated it, both in docs/extraction.md and next to the regex, so each behaviour can be traced to its evidence.
- The golden fixtures (84 pages, tests/fixtures/html/expected.json) pin the full output of each page, authors placed by name, so a change reads as a diff; test_no_page_gives_what_validate_refuses ties the fixtures to the data contract.
- The Extraction diff workflow compares base and head over the daily runs' page cache, and it is really used: 5 runs between 2026-10-28 and 2026-11-02 (extract-v20, extract-v21, aff-38), together with stats --changes, which lists papers losing words first.
- The page cache keeps 1 to 2 KB of front matter that extracts exactly like the whole page (430 local pages in 1.7 MB), which makes offline re-extraction, extraction-diff and new fixtures cheap.
- Extraction is fast: 3.5 s for 430 cached pages (8.1 ms mean, 52 ms at most, a 420-author page included), negligible next to the 15 s crawl delay.
- 100% line and branch coverage, plus has_contact shared by the cleaning and validate, so the cleaning and the contract check agree on what an e-mail is (but see the e-mail fragments finding).
- The other regexes stay linear on long input: _SUBJECT_RE, _ORCID_RE, _CONTACT_TAIL_RE (0.14 s at 9.6 KB) and clean_affiliations on 19 KB of realistic text (0.05 s).

### Items

#### EXT-1 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Exponential backtracking in _MAILBOX_LIST_RE can turn one cached page into a daily hang

- **Problem.** _MAILBOX (arxiv_html.py:205) has two alternatives that overlap: a dotted name like "jane.doe" matches both, and the two separators `\s*[,;|]\s*` and `\s+` overlap too. _MAILBOX_LIST_RE (L206-209) repeats it inside `(?:...)+`. When an unclosed list of k such names is followed by a character none of its endings accept (")", ":", "*"...), the engine tries every split, about 2^k of them.
- **Why it matters.** fetch_front_matter (L755-768) writes the page to the cache before parse_front_matter runs, and collect checks the deadline only between pages (collect.py:339, 902). A hanging page would therefore stall update and reextract until the job times out, and since the page is now cached, every later update, reextract and stats --changes reads it again and stalls again. The rule exists precisely for unclosed lists that run on into another node (2410.23054, 8 items).
- **Change.** Make the alternatives disjoint, or use an atomic group or possessive quantifiers, which Python 3.11+ supports and the project requires 3.13+: `(?>...)` around _MAILBOX and around each separator-plus-item repetition. Add a test that times clean_affiliations on a 40-item unclosed list followed by ")" (it must take under 50 ms). Re-run the golden fixtures and extraction-diff to confirm that nothing else changes. The quadratic _TRAILING_ANCHOR_RE and _OBFUSCATED_EMAIL_RE need no change: node texts are short.
- **Evidence.** Timed by scratch script EXT/redos.py with the input "University of X {" + ", ".join(["a.b"]*k) + " ) x". _MAILBOX_LIST_RE.search: k=14 0.013 s, k=16 0.051 s, k=18 0.21 s, k=20 0.83 s, k=22 3.3 s (four times longer every 2 items; k=30 would take about 15 min). clean_affiliations with 22 realistic mailbox names and a ")" tail: 5.9 s. The same 22 names with the tails "@tum.de", " and others" or ", Apple" take 0.0 s. The largest list in the 430 cached pages is 8 items, unclosed (2410.23054). For comparison, the quadratic regexes take 10.3 s and 13.3 s at 32 KB, but the longest note text in the cache is 983 characters (2608.30657).
- **Related.** AFF-2, AFF-32
- **Verification.** Downgraded, P1 to P2: Reproduced: scratch script verify/redos.py gives _MAILBOX_LIST_RE.search at k=14/16/18/20 = 0.013/0.051/0.204/0.821 s (x4 every 2 items). redos3.py shows clean_affiliation on 'Google Research {name0.last0, ... ,' + 22 dotted names takes 12-13.6 s when the list ends on 'jdoe)', "o'brien}" or 'josé.garcia, Apple'. So the trigger is wider than ')': one accented or apostrophe name inside an unclosed list is enough. A list followed by '} @x.org' or a capitalised affiliation takes 0.0 s. The code matches the finding: arxiv_html.py:205-209 has the overlapping alternatives, :1658 applies the regex, and fetch_front_matter caches the page before parsing (:766-768). Nothing in docs or the roadmap handles backtracking (no grep hit for redos/backtrack). The finding holds, but P1 is not earned. The trigger needs about 20 or more dotted mailbox names in one node, left unclosed or holding a non-ASCII or apostrophe name, with no '@' after them. The largest list in the 431 cached pages has 8 items. The failure would also be loud: a red, timed-out update that names the page being read, which the maintainer would see at once. That makes it a cheap hardening (atomic groups or possessive quantifiers, plus a timing test) at P2, effort S. It is not a likely outage.

#### EXT-3 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: E-mail fragments pass has_contact and validate: 7 strings in 4 papers

- **Problem.** _CONTACT_RE (L194) recognizes an address only by its "@", a link, "(at)" or a brace list. Mailbox names and mail domains printed without the "@" therefore pass the cleaning and validate. A domain gate (_DOMAIN_RE, L509) exists, but only in the note tiers (_footnote_affiliations L1243, _listed_affiliations L1342), not in the shared cleaning.
- **Why it matters.** The data contract says affiliations never contain e-mails, and the repository and site are public. "sarthaktripathi3.gmail.com" is an e-mail address with its @ lost, and validate cannot catch it. #### EXT-4 · P2 · Verified · Medium · M: Defect classes that every rule and every quality sign miss, because rules are tied to one tier

## 8. The site on today's data (WEB)

### Assessment

With about four times the first audit's papers, the site still holds up on a desktop. The heavy costs now come from three things that grow with the data: full-text indexing on the main thread, views that download whole files (abstracts included) when they show only part of them, and the Highly cited "All years" option.

How I measured: production build (`npm run build`, 3.7 s, dist 131 MB of the 800 MB cap), then headless Chromium on an M-series Mac driven by Playwright from my scratch dir. I used 4x CPU throttling as a phone proxy, and a local gzip-6 static server with 4G (9 Mbit/s, 60 ms) and 3G (1.6 Mbit/s, 150 ms) profiles. Search and Rankings were also benchmarked in Node with the real `web/src` modules (vite `ssrLoadModule`).

**Landing page**
- 78.7 kB gzipped of the 100 kB budget (index.js 55.9 kB, CSS 13.4 kB).
- temml (60 kB), WorldMap (49 kB), GraphView (15 kB) and Trends (7.6 kB) all load on demand.
- The default view is **Last week**, not the latest day (`DEFAULT_PERIOD`, period.ts:72): 781 papers, 4 day files plus a citation shard, 2.1 MB raw and 0.74 MB transferred.
- First card: 2.2 s on 4G and 5.3 s on 3G. The latest day alone (0.19 MB) shows its first card in 1.0 s and 2.0 s.
- Last month: 3,731 papers, 3.1 MB gzipped, 3.8 s on 4G and 16.6 s on 3G.
- Highly cited's default (2025): 2.0 MB, 4.2 s on 4G.
- Highly cited "All years": 33 requests, 56.8 MB decoded and 16.4 MB transferred to show 10,000 of 22,831 papers. 17.3 s on 4G, 85 s on 3G, 125 MB of JavaScript heap (192 MB in the graph).
- Every stored paper (`?scope=all`): 80 MB decoded, read file by file with progress.

**Search**
- Index build: the month 1.0 s (+50 MB), all search shards (7,986 papers, no abstracts) 0.46 s (+34 MB), 2024's archive 0.85 s, all 22,831 archived papers 7.3 s (+187 MB).
- Once built, a query takes 0.1 to 10 ms. A phrase with an exclusion costs 50 to 100 ms the first time (475 ms over 22.8k papers).
- The store-wide candidate test takes 0.3 to 0.7 s over 31,817 papers.
- The problem: with a query in the URL, or a query typed before the background build ends, the build runs in one main-thread task. That task lasted 877 ms for a month unthrottled, 5.4 s at 4x CPU, and 14.7 s at 4x CPU for Highly cited all years.

**Graph**
- Month: 5,366 nodes and 7,425 links, settled in about 4.0 s in the worker, 85 MB heap.
- Highly cited all years: 12,417 nodes and 24,601 links, about 4.9 s, longest main-thread task 187 ms.

**Rankings, map and Trends**
- Rankings 330 ms, the map 337 to 417 ms (countries.json 31 kB gzipped), Trends 216 ms (topics.json, 402 kB gzipped, is loaded only by its explorer).
- Rankings by author over every stored paper: 254 MB heap and a 1.0 s main-thread task.

**Accessibility**
- Scanned 9 views at 375 and 1280 px, light and dark:
  - no horizontal overflow;
  - no unnamed buttons or links;
  - no unlabeled fields;
  - no duplicate ids;
  - no images without alt text;
  - no contrast failure on solid backgrounds (gradient-backed marks and medals were not measured).
- Tab paths keep a visible focus ring. Under reduced motion only a 120 ms animation on save remains.
- axe-core is not installed, so it was not run.

**Measured and dismissed:** dropping `abs_url`, `pdf_url`, `provenance` and `extractor` from the archive saves only 3% gzipped (16.1 to 15.6 MB). Abstracts and comments are where the bytes are: 72% of a month's day files gzipped, and 16.1 to 5.0 MB for the archive without them.

### What is good

- The landing code is 78.7 kB gzipped of its 100 kB budget, and every heavy part (graph, world map, Trends, math rendering, store-wide search, About, folders) is a separate chunk loaded on first use.
- Data files are fetched with their version (`?v=`, PERF-8) and kept in a small in-memory cache, and index.json is preloaded and served once (PERF-14): Back or re-choosing a period downloads nothing again.
- Search is fast once the index exists: 0.1 to 10 ms per query and at most 7.4 ms per keystroke over 22.8k papers. Search shards index 7,986 papers without abstracts in 0.46 s.
- The search of every stored paper reads files one by one with read-ahead, keeps only matches in memory (heap 28 MB after scanning 80 MB of JSON), shows progress, and can be cancelled.
- The graph layout runs fully in a Web Worker: 12,417 nodes and 24,601 links settle in about 5 s with no main-thread task above 187 ms.
- No overflow at 375 px in either theme on any of 9 views. No unnamed controls, unlabeled fields or duplicate ids, and no contrast failures on solid backgrounds.
- The world map has a keyboard- and screen-reader-usable table beside it (pin buttons with aria-pressed). The map's zoom buttons, Trends and the folder menu carry proper names and roles, and the folder menu closes on Escape with focus returned.
- Reduced motion is honoured across the views; only a 120 ms save animation remains.

#### WEB-1 · P2 · Verified · Medium · S: A query at load, or typed before the background index ends, freezes the page while the whole period is indexed

- **Problem.** The full-text index is meant to be built in 200-paper chunks while the page is idle (`warm`, started in use-papers.ts:189). But the list's results (`found`, app.tsx:335) call `activeIndex.search` straight away. #### WEB-5 · P2 · Idea · Architectural · L: Abstracts are 72% of what the lists download, yet collapsed by default: load a light list first

- **Problem.** Day files and archive years are served with each paper's abstract and comments, although cards show abstracts only when opened or when 'Show abstracts' is on (decision 19). The abstracts are needed up front only for full-text search. The site already has light records: the search shards, a `light` pointer, and `loadWholePaper`. They are used only for days beyond the paper budget.
- **Why it matters.** Splitting would cut the default week from 0.74 MB to about 0.2 MB, the month from 3.0 to 0.85 MB, and a Highly cited year by about 3x. That is the largest payload saving left and it matters most on phones; it also reduces memory before a search.
- **Change.** Have the build write, for each day file and archive year, a light file (what the search shards hold) plus an abstracts file (id to abstract and comments). The list renders from the light file. Abstracts load in the background, or right away when the URL has a query, and the search index adds the abstract field when they arrive. A card opened before then uses `loadWholePaper` as today. The store's files stay unchanged (the build derives these, as it does the search shards), so the data format is untouched.
- **Evidence.** Script daysplit.py over the last month's day files: 3,731 papers, 3.00 MB gzipped whole, 0.85 MB without abstract and comments (28%). Script lean.py over the archive: 16.1 to 5.0 MB gzipped. Script fields.py: abstracts are 63% of a day file's raw bytes and 59% of archive/2024.json's.
- **Related.** PERF-1, PERF-18, decision 19

#### WEB-6 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Rankings of authors over every stored paper builds a whole graph: 254 MB of heap and a 1 s freeze

- **Problem.** `rankEntities` (rankings.ts:28) builds the full `buildGraph` of papers and entities, with no node cap and authors with one paper included, only to count papers and citations per entity. #### WEB-7 · P3 · Verified · Quick · S: The background index build blocks the main thread for up to 280 ms per chunk on a slow CPU

- **Problem.** `warm` adds `WARM_CHUNK = 200` papers per step (search.ts:345). With abstracts, a chunk takes about 55 ms on an M-series Mac and 220 to 280 ms at 4x CPU, so any tap or keystroke during the build waits that long. The build starts right after the list appears, when people begin to scroll and open cards.
- **Why it matters.** On a mid-range phone, input feels sluggish for the first seconds of every period (a month needs about 19 chunks). The cost grows with the size of each paper's record.
- **Change.** Use a time budget instead of a fixed count: add papers until about 8 to 12 ms have passed, or until `requestIdleCallback`'s `timeRemaining()` runs out, then yield. MiniSearch's `add` works one paper at a time.
- **Evidence.** Playwright at 4x CPU, after the first card: default week, 7 long tasks summing 1,222 ms (longest 259 ms); Last month, 15 long tasks summing 3,207 ms (longest 277 ms). Node: the month's 3,731 papers take 1,010 ms to index, about 54 ms per 200.
- **Related.** PERF-1, PERF-11
