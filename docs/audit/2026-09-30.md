# Preprint Radar: audit and improvement roadmap

*Audit of the repository at commit `e565fbe` (2026-09-30). Analysis only: nothing in the repository was changed. This is a frozen snapshot: line numbers and measurements describe that commit. Progress on its items, and the answers to the decisions of §21, are tracked in [`roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md).*

## How to read this report

- **Scope and method.**
  - Thirteen independent reviews covered the pipeline core, the arXiv sources and extraction, data quality, the citation archive and external sources, storage and growth, search, UI and accessibility, the graph, tests, CI/CD and security, documentation, product and research use, and the git history and architecture.
  - Every claim was checked against the code, and most were measured: offline scripts over all 25,889 stored records, Node benchmarks of the real search and graph code, a production build, and git object statistics.
  - A live walkthrough of the running app followed (desktop, a 375 px phone, dark mode, keyboard).
  - Findings rated P0 or P1 (verified or hypothesis) were then put to three adversarial reviewers each: *does it reproduce*, *is the severity right*, *is it already handled*. Several readers often found the same problem, so one backlog item can rest on up to ten such reviews.
  - The rule: a finding is dropped when the reproduce reviewer refutes it with confidence, or when two reviewers refute it. Every reviewer also proposes a priority. An item's final priority is the majority of all the votes on it, and a tie keeps the original. Findings filed as ideas were not reviewed, and keep the reader's priority.
  - The outcome: the facts held almost everywhere, and two findings were refuted: AFF-4 (a documented design choice, kept as a P3 idea), and a P1 re-rating of the institution-naming split, which stays P2 as AFF-13. The severity reviewer, however, consistently judged the reach smaller than the readers had, on 21 days of data. **The one P0 became P1, and 16 of the 32 P1 items were lowered**, mostly to P2. Each card gives its votes and the reviewers' corrections.
  - Eight completeness reviews then covered what the first pass had missed: the Actions bill, the boundary between day files and the archive, logic duplicated in Python and TypeScript, measured search relevance, the legal and privacy posture, positioning against existing tools, and the first-load budget. The eighth re-estimated effort and dependencies for the P1 and top P2 items.
  - The 440 raw findings (325 from the first pass, 115 from the completeness reviews) were merged into **215 backlog items**.
  - Nothing touched the network except the local dev server.
- **Evidence labels.**
  - **Verified**: confirmed in code (`file:line`) or by a measurement.
  - **Hypothesis**: plausible, with the check that would confirm it.
  - **Idea**: an opportunity, not a defect.
- **Priorities.**
  - **P0**: critical (correctness, security, reliability or a serious architectural problem).
  - **P1**: high value (major user or engineering impact).
  - **P2**: useful.
  - **P3**: exploratory.
- **Effort and kind.**
  - Effort: **S** is under half a day, **M** a day or two, **L** about a week, **XL** more.
  - Kind: **Quick** is a local change. **Medium** touches several modules. **Architectural** changes the data lifecycle, the contract or the runtime structure.
- **Detail.** Every item rated P0 or P1 before verification has a full card (problem, why it matters, change, benefit, risks, dependencies, evidence, verification). Its heading gives the final priority, so some cards are P2 or P3. The other items are in compact tables. §18 lists everything by priority.

## 1. Executive summary

**The verdict.** Preprint Radar is a well-engineered, genuinely useful product, built in six days, and it does not need a rewrite.
- The pipeline respects arXiv's constraints.
- Every write is atomic, and every enrichment is resumable.
- The code is cleanly layered and tested to 100% line and branch coverage offline.
- The web app offers something arXiv does not: affiliations and institutions on every paper, a 16-year highly cited archive, and a graph and Rankings of who works on what.
- The audit found **no security vulnerability** (no XSS path, no secret exposure) and **no data-loss bug in the storage layer**.

**The main weakness is the data lifecycle, not the code.** Every enriched field is fetched once and never re-derived by the code, and nothing checks the data before it is published. As a result, the site drifts away from the code over time:
- Heuristic fixes committed the same day do not reach the papers they were written for. 36 stored strings would be rejected by today's cleaner, and 107 mappings have a shape a fix already forbids.
- A paper revised inside its first days keeps a stale author→institution mapping. 2 papers credit some of their authors to the wrong institution today, silently.
- About 70 affiliation strings are lists of the paper's own authors, mapped onto authors as if they were institutions. A web-side guard keeps all but 2 of them out of the graph, but paper cards, the affiliation filter and the published data show them. The first card of the default view has one.
- Early empty answers from OpenAlex are stored as final, so the 2025 archive year has affiliations for 16% of its papers.
- A change in arXiv's HTML markup would make every new paper "no affiliations", with no retry, in a green run.

The fix is a small set of mechanisms: a `validate` gate, an offline `reclean`, versioned `reextract` with a front-matter cache, and provenance. Together they turn "write once" into "converge" (DATA-1, DATA-3). The readers rated DATA-1 P0. Verification lowered it to P1: nothing is lost, and the maintainer has already replayed fixes by hand twice. What is missing is a command that does it, and a check that says when it is needed. **No P0 remains.**

**Search is fast but imprecise where it matters most for literature review.**
- A clicked author filters by substring: "Hao Li" returns 68 papers, and no author is exactly "Hao Li". A Rankings row reading "Hao Wang, 11 papers" opens 51. Author filters have a mean precision of 0.36.
- Phrases and exclusions ignore authors and affiliations, and a space instead of a hyphen breaks them: `"zero shot"` finds 1 of the 237 zero-shot papers.
- Prefix matching makes "GAN" rank papers by authors named Gan first, and "MIT" match "mitigate".
- The Back button leaves the site.

Each fix is small (SRCH-1 to SRCH-5).

**The client-only design has one scaling cliff.** It loads and indexes every day of a period on the main thread. That is fine at 21 days. At the stated goal of a year, it is about 33 MB gzip and 20 s of indexing per wide period, which will crash phones. It must be fixed before the backfill resumes, with bounded periods and then per-month search shards (PERF-1).

**The product's next leap is the daily workflow.** Keyboard triage with a "seen" state and "since my last visit", saved searches with a watchlist, result export, and BibTeX that compiles would turn the site into a daily habit (PROD-2 to PROD-5). Separately, **eleven months (Oct 2025 – Aug 2026) are in no view**, and the UI does not say so (PROD-1).

**The sources are the right ones, but underused.**
- The weekly Semantic Scholar batch could also return DOIs, published venues and author ids at no extra request (META-1).
- ROR and OpenAlex return institution ids that are thrown away (AFF-14).
- OpenAlex needs a retry policy for young papers (AFF-3).
- Crossref, OpenCitations, Unpaywall, Europe PMC and a direct ORCID integration would add cost without solving a problem here (META-7).

**Scheduled runs degrade silently or expensively.**
- A partial arxiv.org outage burns the whole 3.5-hour step.
- A Semantic Scholar outage on a Sunday can empty `citations.json` for a day. An invalid key leaves it empty until someone notices, after 25 minutes of retries and a green run.
- A rebase conflict would discard a 3-hour run (it has not happened yet).
- There is no run summary, no alert and no symptom-first runbook (REL-1, REL-2, CIT-1, CIT-4, CI-1, CI-2, DOC-1).

**The archive has a latent freeze.** With pruning off (the default), papers stored in day files never compete for the archive. Nothing shows until September 2027, or until the paused backfill resumes, when it starts at once (CIT-11). Fix it before any backfill.

There is also **no license** for the code or the public dataset (REL-3), and **no way for a person the site ranks to ask for a correction** (SEC-9).

**The ten actions with the highest value for their cost:**
1. Enforce the author-mapping invariant and reset it on author changes (DATA-2).
2. Fold accents and punctuation in the author-name filter and drop author-name lists (AFF-1).
3. Add `validate` before every data commit (DATA-3).
4. Add `reclean`, and run it once (DATA-1, layer 1).
5. Fix the archive's failure modes and decouple selection from pruning: merge under the gate, carry missing counts, fix the prune boundary (CIT-1 to CIT-3, CIT-11).
6. Make clicked filters exact, and phrases and exclusions consistent (SRCH-1, SRCH-2).
7. Make the commit step robust, and add a run summary and a failure issue (CI-1, CI-2).
8. Add a drift breaker and a weekly canary for arXiv markup (REL-1).
9. Bound the loaded period (PERF-1, first step).
10. Add an About page with a correction contact, fix BibTeX, and say in the UI what is covered: cs.CV, the dates, and the 11-month gap (SEC-9, PROD-5, PROD-6, PROD-1).

**Backlog after verification:** 0 P0, 20 P1, 115 P2 and 80 P3 items (§18). The roadmap (§19) takes 1–2 days to stop publishing wrong data, about a week to make the pipeline self-healing and observable, 1–2 weeks for search precision and the daily workflow, then 2–4 weeks of scaling and richer metadata. Twenty decisions need your input (§21). The most consequential are retention and the 11-month gap, pruning, the handling of revised papers, the license, and the primary persona.

## 2. Current architecture and how the system works

### 2.1 The whole system on one page

```
 GitHub Actions (private repo)                                  GitHub Pages (public site)
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐   ┌──────────────────────────────┐
 │ update.yml   Mon–Fri 06:17 UTC (often starts ~10:00)     │   │ web/dist (Vite build)        │
 │   preprint-radar update  → listing, abstracts, HTML,     │   │   index.html + 116 kB JS     │
 │                            ROR, day files                │   │   data/ copied verbatim      │
 │   preprint-radar citations → S2 counts of new papers     │   │   (58 MB, no hashes)         │
 │   git commit → pull --rebase → push → deploy.yml         │   │                              │
 │ archive.yml  Sun 05:41 UTC                               │   │ Preact + MiniSearch +        │
 │   preprint-radar archive → OAI catalog (Actions cache),  │──▶│ d3-force canvas, all         │
 │     S2 counts (207k ids), selection, OpenAlex, archive/  │   │ client-side:                 │
 │ ci.yml       lint, types, tests, build (not on bot data) │   │ Papers · Highly cited ·      │
 │ deploy.yml   npm ci, build, upload Pages artifact        │   │ Graph · Rankings · Reading   │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘   │ list (localStorage)          │
           │ commits data/ to main (git is the database)        └──────────────────────────────┘
           ▼
 data/index.json · data/cs.CV/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json (21 days) · data/cs.CV/archive/<YYYY>.json (16 years)
 · data/cs.CV/citations.json (25,723 counts)
```

The project is six days old (77 commits from 2026-09-25 to 2026-09-30, 73 by the maintainer with AI co-authors, 4 by the bot). It is already a complete product. There is no server: the pipeline writes JSON, git stores it, and Pages serves it to a client that does everything in the browser.

### 2.2 Pipeline (Python 3.13+, `src/preprint_radar/`)

- **Layering.** `cli.py` dispatches seven commands (`fetch`, `update`, `backfill`, `stats`, `institutions`, `archive`, `citations`). `collect.py` orchestrates the daily path. `archive/` runs the weekly citation archive. `sources/` has one module per external service, each split into fetch and pure parse. `store/` owns the `data/` layout. Dependencies point one way (cli → collect/archive → sources + store), with only three source-to-source edges and no cycles.
- **HTTP.** Every request goes through `http.HttpClient`: one client per service, a per-instance rate limiter measured between request starts, retries on transport errors and on 406/429/5xx (4 attempts, 10 s doubling, numeric `Retry-After` capped at 300 s), 404 mapped to `NotFoundError`. The header set is reduced to `User-Agent` and `Accept`, because export.arxiv.org's firewall rejects httpx's defaults. The contact e-mail reaches arXiv clients only.
- **Daily path (`update_category`, collect.py:49-111).**
  1. The recent listing (`/list/cs.CV/recent`, about 5 announcement days) gives titles, authors, subjects, comments, `journal_ref`, the HTML link and a venue parsed from comments.
  2. `_carry_over` merges each listed paper with its stored copy. Listing metadata is fresh, while `abstract`, `affiliations`, `author_affiliations` and `institutions` are copied from the store.
  3. Abstracts come from RSS (one request), then catchup pages (one per day), then the Atom API (which stops at the first failed batch), then the HTML page. The day files are saved at this point.
  4. HTML front matter is read for papers whose `affiliations is None` and that have an `html_url`, at the 15 s crawl delay. The limit is `--max-html` (600 in CI), with a checkpoint every 20 pages.
  5. ROR names the institution of each distinct affiliation string (at most 1,000 lookups a run, stopping after 3 consecutive failures).
  6. The final save follows, and the CLI rewrites `index.json` after all categories.
- **Backfill** walks catchup pages newest first, saves each day before the slow HTML phase and writes the index early.
- **Retry contract.** `null` means "not retrieved yet" and is retried. `[]` means "tried, nothing found" and is final. Every enrichment step acts only on `null` fields, which makes each step idempotent and resumable.
- **Affiliation extraction (`sources/arxiv_html.py`, 694 lines, 8 commits in 6 days).**
  - Four tiers produce candidate strings, in order: tagged `ltx_role_affiliation/address` contacts and address/institute notes, then numbered lines, then "A is with B" thanks sentences.
  - `clean_affiliation` then removes contact details, LaTeX spacing tokens and link labels.
  - A three-tier author mapping (per-creator contacts, superscript markers, "is with" sentences) cancels a tier on any ambiguity, following the rule that "a wrong pair is worse than none".

### 2.3 Weekly citation archive (`archive/`)

1. `archive/catalog.py` keeps the whole OAI-PMH set `cs:cs:CV` (207,468 records, 112 MB gzip) in `.cache/catalog/`. The catalog is restored from the Actions cache and harvested incrementally, with resumable gzip members.
2. `sources/semantic_scholar.py` asks for about 207,000 citation counts in 415 POSTs of 500 ids. Each batch is retried up to 30 times, and a 7-day JSONL cache lets a cancelled run resume.
3. `archive/selection.py` ranks papers older than 365 days by citations per year of age (`max(age, 1)`). It keeps a budget of 22,000, with hysteresis up to 25,300. The threshold today is 19.54 citations a year.
4. Entering papers get institutions from OpenAlex, looked up by the arXiv DOI.
5. Below 95% citation coverage, the selection is frozen.
6. The archive years are then rewritten, followed by `citations.json` (counts of every stored paper) and the index.

### 2.4 Data layout and contract

- **Files.** `data/<cat>/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json` holds one file per announcement day. `archive/<YYYY>.json` holds one file per year of first version. `citations.json` is a sorted map kept out of the records, so that the weekly refresh rewrites one file. `index.json` gives the days, counts and archive years. Writes are atomic (temp file plus `replace`), and output is deterministic and diff-friendly.
- **The record.** It is `Paper.to_record()` in `models.py`, mirrored in `web/src/types.ts`. `schema_version` is 1.
  - `author_affiliations` is a positional parallel array (author i → indices into `affiliations`).
  - `institutions` is parallel to `affiliations`.
  - Optional fields can be added without a schema bump.
- **What the record does not carry.** It has no paper version, DOI, provenance, extraction date or withdrawal flag, and no identifier for authors or institutions (see sections 6 and 12).

### 2.5 Web app (`web/`, Preact + TypeScript strict, 5,102 lines)

- **Loading.**
  - `usePapers` loads `index.json`, then either the day files of the selected period (six in parallel) or one archive year.
  - `citations.json` is loaded once per category.
  - A `PaperIndex` (MiniSearch) is built in the browser over title ×3, authors ×2, affiliations and derived institutions ×1.5, venue ×1.5, abstract ×1 and comments ×0.5. Matching uses prefix on every term, fuzzy matching from 5 characters, and AND between terms.
- **Query syntax.** Quoted phrases and `-exclusions` are applied as post-filters.
- **Structured filters.** Author, affiliation, venue, primary subject and a cross-list toggle.
- **State.** All state lives in the URL (`url-state.ts`) and is written with `history.replaceState`.
- **Views.**
  - Papers: day folding, relevance, newest or most-cited sort, 40 cards at a time.
  - Highly cited: one archive year.
  - Rankings: institutions or authors, merged exactly like graph nodes.
  - Graph: a full-screen d3-force canvas of papers, institutions and authors, with a co-occurrence projection.
  - Reading list: localStorage, with BibTeX and JSON export.
- **Institution naming.** It is split in two. The pipeline stores ROR names (about 66% of recent strings), and `web/src/institutions.ts` names the rest at runtime with acronym and brand rules.

### 2.6 CI/CD

- `ci.yml` runs ruff, ty, actionlint and pytest on Python 3.13 and 3.14, then Biome, tsc, Vitest and a build on Node 24.
- `update.yml` and `archive.yml` share a concurrency group, check out `main` (not the trigger SHA) and commit data with `GITHUB_TOKEN`. Because a `GITHUB_TOKEN` push triggers no other workflow, they call `deploy.yml` through `workflow_call`, so bot data commits never go through `ci.yml`.
- Actions are pinned by SHA, installs are locked, and Dependabot covers all three ecosystems.

## 3. What is already good

This is a well-built project, and most of it should stay as it is. The audit found no reason to rewrite or replace any technology. The strengths below are verified, and several are rare in projects of this size and age.

- **Evidence-driven engineering.** Commit bodies carry arXiv ids of failing pages, before/after numbers (df2661f: 3,025 → 2,767 institution nodes) and rejected alternatives. The archive policy was calibrated in a separate experiment, with S2 against OpenAlex and a budget measured on distributions.
- **Polite, constraint-aware crawling.** The 15 s robots.txt delay, the export.arxiv.org header and comma quirks, and the RSS → catchup → API order are encoded where they bite, each with its reason in a comment. The contact e-mail never leaves arXiv clients, and forks default to the repository URL.
- **Crash safety and resumability.**
  - Every JSON write is atomic, and HTML progress is checkpointed every 20 pages.
  - The OAI harvest resumes page by page and repairs a truncated gzip member.
  - The S2 counts cache lets a cancelled weekly run resume.
  - Enrichment is idempotent through the `null`/`[]` contract.
- **Graceful degradation.**
  - An RSS failure is only a warning, and a failed catchup day is skipped in `update`.
  - The API stops at the first failed batch instead of prolonging a throttle.
  - ROR stops after 3 failures and never fails the run.
  - `citations.json` is replaced only when every batch answered in the daily path.
- **Test and static-analysis discipline.** 336 offline pytest tests run in 1.8 s at 100% line and branch coverage (2,040 statements, 508 branches). The HTTP boundary is faked with `MockTransport` and injected clocks. 23 real, trimmed HTML fixtures back one named case per heuristic. ruff `ALL` has only 3 justified `noqa`. ty runs with error-on-warning, and tsc is strict with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess` and `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`.
- **Clean module boundaries.** `store/paper_store.py` imports only `models` and `exceptions`. `collect.py` never touches BeautifulSoup, and sources return typed results. Service limits live next to their reason, and tunables live in `config.py`.
- **Diff-friendly, bounded storage.**
  - Citation counts sit in one sorted file, so the whole weekly archive commit pushed only 97 KB.
  - A daily update costs about 730 bytes of packed history per new paper.
  - The archive has a fixed size budget.
  - The packed repository is 16.8 MB, although the working tree holds 58 MB of data.
- **Safe rendering.** No `innerHTML` or `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` appears in `web/src`. `linkify` can only produce `http(s)://` hrefs, and every `target="_blank"` link has `rel="noopener noreferrer"`. The site is XSS-safe today.
- **Product fundamentals.**
  - Everything is in the URL, so every search is bookmarkable.
  - Relevance is explained in the UI.
  - Clicking an author or affiliation filters by it.
  - Rankings and the graph share one naming model.
  - The graph degrades honestly: it explains why a paper is absent.
  - BibTeX follows arXiv's `@misc`/`eprint` convention.
  - The footer credits every source, with arXiv's required acknowledgement.
- **Accessibility baseline.**
  - Buttons are real buttons, with native selects and `details`, and a real `<table>` in Rankings.
  - `aria-pressed` and `aria-expanded` are set where they apply, and the result count is a live region.
  - The period picker's Escape handling returns focus to its trigger.
  - `prefers-reduced-motion` is honored.
  - The main text passes AA contrast in both themes, and the two dark palette blocks are identical, as CLAUDE.md requires.
- **Supply chain.** Actions are pinned by commit SHA. `uv sync --locked` and `npm ci` are used everywhere, and all 168 npm and 26 PyPI resolutions come from the official registries. Only local and builtin git hooks are used, and there is no `curl | sh`.

## 4. Problems and technical debt

Most findings trace back to five cross-cutting causes. Fixing a cause fixes many findings at once, so this section starts with the causes and then gives the detail.

| Root cause | What it produces | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Enrichment is write-once.** A field is fetched once, when it is `null`, and never re-derived | Parser fixes do not reach stored papers. Revised papers keep stale author mappings. OpenAlex's early empty answers become final. A layout change would make every new paper permanently empty | §4.1, §8, §13 |
| **Nothing checks the data before it is published** | Two contract violations and dozens of junk strings are deployed. Bot commits skip CI entirely | §4.1, §14, §15 |
| **Scheduled runs fail silently or expensively** | Green runs with degraded data. A rebase conflict would discard a 3-hour run. A partial arXiv outage burns the whole step. An invalid S2 key costs 25 minutes and exits 0 | §13, §15 |
| **The client loads and indexes everything on the main thread** | Fine at 21 days, but breaks at the stated goal of a year: about 33 MB gzip and about 20 s of indexing per wide period | §11 |
| **Search semantics were tuned for recall, not precision** | Substring filters return strangers, acronyms match names, and phrases and exclusions ignore half the fields | §9 |

### 4.1 Data integrity

#### DATA-1 · P1 · Verified · Architectural · M: Extractor fixes never reach stored data, and stored data cannot be re-derived from the code

- **Problem.** The HTML front matter is read only while `affiliations is None` (`collect.py:288`). No code ever resets that field, and the record carries no extractor version. No command re-runs `clean_affiliation` or the author-mapping tiers over stored papers, and the parsed HTML is discarded (`arxiv_html.py:126-145`). Every heuristic fix therefore applies only to papers announced after it. The five fix commits to `arxiv_html.py` (3212614, 08c4dbf, 1a63530, 8996502, 743e1c8) did not change the stored papers they were written for. Earlier re-extractions (0ec40be, 77c7be6, 0f337ec, d5cf44f) came from local scripts that are not in the repository: 77c7be6 set 255 papers back to `null` so that the next backfill re-read them, and 0f337ec re-read 50 pages after a fix.
- **Evidence (measured over `data/`).**
  - Today's `clean_affiliation` rejects 36 stored strings (31 papers) and rewrites 5. Examples: `'[0.2em]'`, `'Correspondence'`, `'Project page'`, and `'MITProject page'` → `'MIT'`.
  - 2609.38153 is itself the fixture of the `[5pt]` rule, yet it is stored as `['[5pt]', 'Johns Hopkins University', '[10pt]', 'Project Website']`. Its test expects `('Johns Hopkins University',)`.
  - 107 papers still have the "only the last author holds every affiliation" shape that 743e1c8 fixed. 2609.00866, the paper CLAUDE.md cites as fixed, still gives its 25 affiliations to its 55th author.
  - Of the 23 fixtures that are stored papers, 4 are stored differently from what today's parser produces.
- **Why it matters.** The maintainer spends hours on heuristics whose effect never reaches the site. `make stats` measures the old parser, not the current one. Wrong author-institution pairs stay in the graph and Rankings, although the contract calls them "worse than none". Whether `data/` matches the code depends on unrecorded manual operations.
- **Change (three layers, each useful on its own).**
  1. *`preprint-radar reclean` (offline, S).*
     - It re-runs `clean_affiliation` over every stored string and dedupes the result.
     - It remaps `author_affiliations` through an old → new index table, and an entry that ends up empty becomes `null`.
     - It applies the last-creator rule offline.
     - It sets `institutions` to `null` only when the strings changed, so ROR re-runs.
     - It fixes the 36 strings and 107 mappings above in seconds, with no network.
  2. *Versioned re-extraction (M).*
     - Add `EXTRACTOR_VERSION` in `arxiv_html.py`, bumped by any heuristic change, and an optional record field `extractor` (optional, so no schema bump).
     - Add `preprint-radar reextract [--ids …] [--match REGEX] [--below-version N]`. It resets the selected papers to `null` and runs the existing `_add_html_front_matter` path, with the same rate limit and checkpoints.
     - Give it a small daily budget in `update.yml`, for example 50 pages, so fixes converge without human data commits.
  3. *Front-matter cache (M).*
     - Keep a gzipped extract of each page read: `div.ltx_authors`, the address, institutetext and thanks notes, the top-level footnotetext and the abstract. That is a few KB per paper, keyed by `arxiv_id` and version.
     - `reextract` then becomes offline, and every heuristic change can be evaluated on thousands of real pages before shipping.
- **Benefit.** `data/` becomes reproducible from code plus arXiv. Heuristic work reaches the whole rolling year within minutes (reclean) or days (reextract), and the one-off data scripts disappear.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Index remapping must stay consistent with the parallel `institutions` list, so unit-test it on a record where a middle string is dropped. A full re-read costs 15 s a page (about 15 h for 3,634 papers), so re-extraction must be selective or capped. The cache location is a decision (§21). The Actions cache is evicted after 7 idle days, while a data branch or a release asset is durable. The cache stores third-party HTML fragments, but only the front matter, which the site already republishes in parsed form. Keep `reclean` away from archive affiliations: they are OpenAlex institution names, and the cleaner would damage 11 of 27,231 ('IIT@MIT', 'CNRS@CREATE Ltd', '9to5' → 'to5').
- **Dependencies.** DATA-3 (validation) to measure the effect. Layers 1 → 2 → 3 build on each other, and layer 1 alone already repairs today's data.
- **Verification.** Reproduced in five independent reviews (15 votes). No reviewer accepted P0: 9 votes for P1 and 6 for P2, so it is **P1**. Their main correction: a fix *can* reach stored papers, because setting `affiliations` back to `null` re-queues a paper, and the maintainer has done it twice. What is missing is a command that selects the papers, and a check that says when to run it.

#### DATA-2 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Author mappings go out of step with their authors when a paper is revised inside the listing window

- **Problem.** `_carry_over` (`collect.py:195-208`) takes `authors` fresh from the listing but copies the positional `author_affiliations` from the store. When a v2 reorders, inserts or removes authors while the paper is still in the 5-day recent listing, index *i* now points at someone else. Nothing re-reads the page, since `affiliations` is not `null`. The same bug class exists in `_add_abstracts` (`collect.py:254-257`): API affiliations replace the list but keep the old mapping. `Paper.from_record` checks that `institutions` is parallel to `affiliations`, but it never checks `author_affiliations` against `authors`. It also accepts out-of-range and boolean indices (`models.py:208-227`).
- **Evidence.**
  - Two stored records violate the contract: 2609.27511 has 18 authors and 16 entries, and 2609.34658 has 9 authors and 8 entries.
  - Replaying git history over the day files finds 6 author-list changes that carried a mapping. 2 of them misattribute. In 2609.27511 (16 → 18 authors), the two inserted authors get NVIDIA and two others lose their place. In 2609.34658 (8 → 9), the inserted Zeqiang Lai gets USTC, while Xiaoxiao Ma and Yuhang Zang lose theirs. A third, 2609.27682, is a reorder that its single affiliation makes harmless.
  - `web/src/graph.ts:137` reads `author_affiliations?.[position]` without a length check.
- **Why it matters.** The graph and Rankings publicly credit people to the wrong institution. The error is silent, and `stats` counts the paper as mapped. It recurs whenever a paper is revised within its first days. That is rare today (about 1 paper in 1,000 measured), but systematic.
- **Change.**
  1. In `_carry_over`, compare author lists after folding. When they differ beyond a pure rename, reset `affiliations`, `author_affiliations` and `institutions` to `null`, so the new version is read (one 15 s page per revised paper, a handful a day). The cheaper option is to drop only the mapping, but it is then never retried.
  2. In `_add_abstracts`, reset `author_affiliations` whenever the list is replaced.
  3. Enforce the invariants in `Paper` itself (a `__post_init__` or `from_record`): one entry per author, indices within `affiliations`, no booleans.
  4. Add a length guard in `graph.ts`.
  5. Repair the two records in the same commit, since otherwise the stricter loader refuses the store.
  6. Add a `test_collect.py` case with a reordered author list.
- **Benefit.** The "no wrong pair" guarantee is restored, and the contract the web relies on is enforced where records are read.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A strict check before the repair breaks the next run. Name-only renames ("Ma Ziqi" → "Ziqi Ma", 3 of the 6 historical cases) should keep their mapping, so compare folded names order-sensitively.
- **Dependencies.** None. It pairs with DATA-3.
- **Verification.** Reproduced end to end: two `update_category` runs on a temporary store, the second with a reordered and extended author list, read no HTML and kept the old mapping. Seven readers found this bug independently, and three more reviews covered the invariant: 10 votes for P1 and 20 for P2, so it is **P2**. It is rare (2 papers in 21 days), but it stays a first-phase quick win (§19), because the damage is silent and public and the fix takes about an hour.

#### DATA-3 · P1 · Verified · Medium · M: Nothing validates `data/` between the pipeline and the public site

- **Problem.** No test reads the committed `data/`: every test uses `tmp_path`. Bot pushes use `GITHUB_TOKEN`, so they never trigger `ci.yml`, and `deploy.yml` only builds. Between a file being written and it being served, nothing checks the contract, the invariants or the parser guarantees.
- **Evidence.** A prototype checker loaded all 37 files (25,889 papers) through `Paper.from_record` in about 2 s. It found 2 mapping length mismatches, 2-3 affiliations equal to an author name, 6 LaTeX spacing tokens, 4 obfuscated e-mail strings, 2 ids stored on two days, and 41 strings that are not fixed points of the current cleaner. All of them are deployed.
- **Why it matters.** Each of these defects would have been caught by a one-second offline check. For a single maintainer, a machine gate is the only realistic quality control on unattended runs.
- **Change.** Add `store/validate.py` and a `preprint-radar validate` command (and `make validate`).
  - *Structural checks* fail the run: `from_record` round-trip, `schema_version`, file stem equal to `announced` (days) or the year (archive), mapping length and index range, `institutions` length, unique ids across day files, `index.json` counts equal to file contents, `citations.json` ids a subset of stored ids, and no e-mails or URLs in affiliations.
  - *Heuristic checks* warn: an affiliation equal to an author name, a string that is not a fixed point of `clean_affiliation`, and the last-author-holds-all shape.
  - Run it in `update.yml` and `archive.yml` before *Commit data*. On failure, keep the data as an artifact instead of pushing it. Run it in `ci.yml` on every push, and print the warnings in the step summary.
- **Benefit.** Bad data is caught where it enters. The warnings become the affiliation-quality dashboard the heuristics work lacks (see AFF-Q in §8).
- **Risks and trade-offs.** *Commit data* runs with `if: always()` in `update.yml`, so a failing step placed before it would not stop the push: give the validate step an `id` and push only when it passed. A strict gate can block a day's deploy, so start with warnings and promote rule by rule. Keep `validate` out of `make test`, which must not depend on the data.
- **Dependencies.** DATA-2 (repair first). It enables DATA-1 measurement.
- **Verification.** Reproduced (4 votes for P1, 2 for P2), so it stays **P1**. Partial checks exist: every run ends with `write_index()`, which loads each file through `Paper.from_record`, and the web rejects a wrong `schema_version`. Neither checks the mapping invariants or the cleaner's guarantees.

### 5.1 Who it serves today

### 5.2 High-value product items

#### PROD-1 · P1 · Verified · Medium · M: Papers from 2025-10-01 to 2026-08-31 are in no view, and the UI does not say so

- **Problem.** The archive only admits papers older than 365 days (`config.py:36`), and the day files cover only September 2026, because the backfill is paused. Eleven months of cs.CV, the most relevant for related work, are neither searchable nor ranked. The catalog already holds 35,030 of these papers.
- **Why it matters.** A survey writer searching "gaussian splatting" gets this month plus the classics, but not last winter's key papers. The period picker shows the stored span, but nothing names the hole or says where else to look.
- **Change.**
  1. *Now (S).* Add one line under the period picker: "Recent: 1–30 Sept 2026. Older highly cited papers: Highly cited tab. Anything else: search arXiv", with a link.
  2. *Then (M, decision in §21).*
     - Resume the HTML backfill to 12 months. That costs about 11 h of CI per month of cs.CV.
     - Or write day files for the gap straight from the OAI catalog, with abstracts and no affiliations. That adds about 88 MB and no HTML crawl, and the HTML pass fills affiliations later within the daily budget.
     - Either way, bounded loading (PERF-1) must land first.
- **Benefit.** Users are not misled. After the fill, the day files and the archive meet at 365 days, and the product covers a continuous history.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Repository and site size grow (see §12, retention). The catalog route gives approximate announcement dates (META-5).
- **Dependencies.** PERF-1, and the retention decision in §21.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (5 votes of 6). Correction: the picker shows the span and the paper count, and its calendar stops at September 2026. What is missing is a sentence that names the gap.

#### PROD-2 · P1 · Verified · Medium · M: Daily triage has no keyboard path, no "seen" state and no "since my last visit"

- **Problem.** Keyboard handling exists only in the period picker and the graph. There is no j/k navigation, no "mark seen", no "hide seen", and no memory of the last visit. Tomorrow's default period, "Last 5 days", re-shows four days already triaged.
- **Why it matters.** Without these, triaging 90–564 papers a day (median 128) is slower than skimming arXiv's own listing, and this is the core persona.
- **Change.**
  - In `PaperList`, keep a focused index, with this keymap: j/k move and scroll, o or Enter expands, s stars, x marks seen, / focuses search. Reuse `GraphView`'s is-typing guard.
  - Store seen ids per category in `localStorage`, pruned to stored ids. Dim seen cards and add a "Hide seen" toggle.
  - Record the newest day rendered, and add a "Since last visit" range with an "N new papers since Fri" badge. Make it the default for returning visitors.
  - Never encode the last-visit value in the URL, so shared links stay neutral.
- **Benefit.** Several times faster triage, and a reason to come back every day.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** The state is per browser. The seen set grows by about 130 ids a day, so prune it.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (2 votes of 3). Correction: card controls are native buttons and links, so Tab reaches them. What is missing is triage shortcuts: a collapsed card has a median of 14 tab stops.

#### PROD-3 · P2 · Verified · Medium · M: Saved searches and a watchlist

- **Problem.** Bookmarks already give each topic's newest papers, because the URL holds the state and the period presets are relative. What the app cannot do is say "topic A: 12 new, lab B: 3 new" in one place. The name "Radar" promises exactly that.
- **Change.** Add `saved-searches.ts`: named `writeState` query strings in `localStorage`, created with a "Save this search" button beside the result count. A Watchlist panel runs each saved query against the loaded index for "since last visit". `PaperIndex.search` takes about 1 ms at this size, so this is cheap. Add the saved searches to the reading list's existing JSON export, and add an import (PROD-4).
- **Benefit.** Tracking without a server, reusing the existing index and URL format.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** It is per browser, and it only covers loaded days.
- **Dependencies.** PROD-2 ("since last visit"), PROD-4 (import/export).
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 2 for P2): bookmarks of relative-period URLs already cover most of the need. It is differentiating only when a saved search carries an affiliation, author or venue filter (§17).

#### PROD-4 · P2 · Verified · Medium · M: The reading list can silently lose papers and cannot be moved or restored

- **Problem.** The reading list is the only persistent user asset, and it has several gaps:
  1. JSON export exists, but there is no import.
  2. `saveReadingList` swallows `QuotaExceededError`: the star turns on, and the paper is gone on reload.
  3. With no `storage` listener, two tabs overwrite each other's additions.
  4. The stored array is unversioned and cast without validation. A malformed entry throws inside `PaperIndex`, and there is no error boundary, so the page likely goes blank.
  5. Records are frozen at save time, so a paper starred before its HTML pass keeps `affiliations: null` forever.
- **Change.**
  - Add Import (JSON, merged by id, validated with an `isPaper` guard).
  - Store `{version: 1, papers}` with migration from the bare array.
  - Surface quota errors, listen to `storage` events, and refresh saved records from any loaded day that contains them.
  - Optionally add per-paper notes and tags in a separate key, which a survey writer needs to group papers by section.
- **Benefit.** A portable, recoverable list, safe across tabs, that gains later enrichment.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Imports are untrusted input, so validate types and keep the feature purely local.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 2 for P2). The quota loss needs about 2,200 saved papers, so it is theoretical, and the two-tab overwrite is the realistic silent loss. Citation counts of saved papers are looked up live. A malformed entry would throw at startup in every view, since `useReadingList` builds its index on the first render.

#### PROD-5 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: BibTeX export can break LaTeX compilation and cite the wrong paper

- **Problem.** `bibtex.ts` writes titles verbatim.
  - `&`, `%`, `_` and `^` are not escaped outside math. `&`, `_` and `^` stop the compile with an error (Overleaf's nonstop mode still produces a PDF), and `%` silently cuts the title short: "0.5%>100%: …" becomes "0.5segmentation". 79 stored titles have `&`, and 12 have `_` or `^`.
  - Single braces let most `.bst` styles lowercase NeRF, CLIP and 3D.
  - Keys (first author's surname + year + first word) collide: 40 keys among the 3,816 recent papers, and 351 in the archive. `toBibtexFile` does not disambiguate them. BibTeX reports a "Repeated entry" and keeps the first, so both citations point at one paper.
  - There is no arXiv DOI, no venue, and "Jr." or "III" becomes the surname.
- **Change.**
  - Add `escapeBibtex()` for `& % # _ ^` outside `$…$`, skipping characters already escaped.
  - Wrap titles in `{{…}}`, or brace only mixed-case and acronym words (decision in §21).
  - Suffix repeated keys with a/b/c after sorting by `arxiv_id`, and skip Jr./Sr./II/III when picking the surname.
  - Add `doi = {10.48550/arXiv.<id>}` and `note = {Accepted to <venue>}`.
  - Add a test for each rule, using the real titles as fixtures.
- **Benefit.** Exported bibliographies compile and cite the right papers.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Double-braced titles block sentence-case styles, which some people want.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (2 votes for P1, 7 for P2): a PDF is still produced, and a repeated key is reported. The silent failures are `%` and the collisions.

## 6. Literature, paper and metadata strategy

### 6.1 Assessment of the current source strategy

The current choices are sound and mostly evidence-based.
- arXiv's own channels are used for everything arXiv is authoritative for: listing, catchup, RSS and the API, plus OAI-PMH in bulk.
- Semantic Scholar supplies counts, and ROR and OpenAlex supply institutions.

The weaknesses are not the choice of sources but **what is kept from them and for how long**.
- Only the fields needed on day one are stored.
- Nothing is refreshed after the listing window.
- Identifiers that sources already return (ROR ids, DOIs, author ids) are discarded.
- No field records where it came from or when.

### 6.2 Source authority and recommended precedence

| Field | Today | Authority | Recommended strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title, authors, abstract, subjects, comments, `journal_ref` | arXiv listing/RSS/catchup (days), OAI (archive); never refreshed | arXiv (for the arXiv version) | Keep. Refresh weekly from the OAI catalog that `archive` already harvests: comments, `journal_ref`, the venue recomputed offline, and withdrawals (META-2) |
| Version, withdrawn | Not stored (`html_url` may say v2; 260 recent papers are v2+). 473 catalog entries mention a withdrawal, and 8 are archived | arXiv | Add optional `version` and `withdrawn`, from the listing link and the catalog comments. Re-read HTML only when the author list changed (DATA-2) |
| arXiv DOI | Not stored | Deterministic: `10.48550/arXiv.<id>` | Derive it; no request needed. Emit it in BibTeX, RIS and the data |
| Published DOI and venue | Not stored. Venue is regex-derived from comments: 20% of recent papers, 52% of the archive | Publisher (Crossref); S2 and OpenAlex aggregate it | Take `externalIds.DOI`, `publicationVenue` and `venue` from the **Semantic Scholar batch already made weekly** (META-1). Keep the regex for fresh papers, where S2 has nothing yet. Crossref is not needed |
| Affiliations | API submitter field (rare), then HTML heuristics (80% of tried recent papers), then OpenAlex institutions for archive entrants (16–60%) | The paper itself; OpenAlex for published records | Keep HTML as primary for recent papers. Give `[]` a retry policy for OpenAlex (AFF-3). Spike the published-version OpenAlex record and the OAI `arXiv` format's `<affiliation>` (§8) |
| Institution identity | ROR name only (the id is dropped); web heuristics for the rest; OpenAlex returns ROR ids that are thrown away | ROR | Store `institution_ids` (ROR ids) parallel to `institutions`. Key the web by id when present. Use ROR relationships for rollups (§8) |
| Author identity | Name strings; homonyms merged ("Wei Wang": 16 papers at 11 institutions) | ORCID, where present; S2 and OpenAlex author ids | Add a homonym hint in the web first. Then take S2 `authors.authorId` from the same weekly batch for stored papers (spike). OpenAlex author ids and ORCID are already fetched for archive entrants and discarded. ORCID alone covers too few arXiv authors to be the key |
| Citation counts | S2 `citationCount` (counts all versions), weekly plus a daily merge | No single authority; S2 is the best free aggregate for CS preprints | Keep S2. Do not add OpenAlex counts (×1.7 undercount measured) or OpenCitations (built on Crossref DOI references, so it misses citations to arXiv-only preprints) |
| Code availability | Not stored; GitHub links sit in the text | The paper | Derive it client-side (PROD-12); no external source |

### 6.3 Design principles to adopt

1. **Every enriched field says where it came from and when.**
   - Add an optional `provenance` map, for example `{"affiliations": {"source": "html", "at": "2026-09-29", "extractor": 7}, "institutions": {"source": "ror"}, "venue": {"source": "comments"}}`.
   - This makes conflicts explainable, retries selective and audits possible. Optional fields need no schema bump under the data contract.
2. **Three states instead of two.**
   - `null` means "not tried".
   - `[]` means "tried, nothing".
   - The third state is "tried, nothing yet; retry after D", for sources whose coverage grows with age (OpenAlex, S2 for new papers).
   - Encode it through `provenance.at` plus a per-source retry rule, not a new sentinel value.
3. **Precedence rules are written down and applied in one place.**
   - Affiliations: submitter-declared, then HTML tagged/notes, then HTML numbered or thanks tiers, then OpenAlex.
   - Institution naming: a ROR id beats a ROR name, which beats the heuristic name.
   - Venue: S2 `publicationVenue`, when it is a real venue, beats the comment regex, which beats nothing.
   - Code the precedence in the enrichment step interface (DEBT-1), not scattered across `collect.py` and `archive/update.py`.
4. **Data that changes weekly goes in sidecar files keyed by id**, as `citations.json` already does. That covers S2 DOI, venue and author ids (`s2.json`). The paper files then stay stable, and git and Pages churn stays small.
5. **Identifiers over names.** Store ids wherever a source provides them (ROR, DOI, S2 or OpenAlex author ids). Names are for display, and ids are for merging.
6. **Freshness is explicit.** Show the date of counts on the page, not in a tooltip. Flag counts older than 14 days as stale (CIT-4).

### 6.4 Items

#### META-1 · P1 · Idea · Medium · M: Get DOI, published venue and author ids from the Semantic Scholar batch already made every week

- **Problem.** `_fetch_batch` asks only `fields=citationCount` (`semantic_scholar.py:135`), in 415 requests a week. The same endpoint returns `externalIds` (the published DOI, DBLP), `venue`, `publicationVenue`, `publicationDate` and `authors.authorId`. The store has no DOI at all, and venue detection depends on comment regexes, which miss most published papers (48% of the archive has no venue).
- **Why it matters.** Three real gaps close at no new service, key or rate budget:
  - a published venue for archived papers,
  - a DOI for BibTeX and links,
  - stable author ids for disambiguation (AFF-H).
- **Change.**
  1. Request the extra fields only for stored ids: about 26k ids, 52 of the 415 requests.
  2. Parse them into a small dataclass per paper.
  3. Write `data/<cat>/s2.json` (`{id: {doi, venue, author_ids}}`), so paper files are not rewritten.
  4. Add optional web fields `doi?`, `published_venue?` and `author_ids?`.
  5. Prefer S2's venue over the regex when it is a real venue (not "arXiv.org"), normalized through the venue table (META-4).
- **Benefit.** DOI and venue for most archived papers, and the basis for author disambiguation, for about 0 extra requests.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Larger responses may raise 429s or time out. S2 venue strings are noisy. **Spike first:** one keyed batch of 500 stored ids with those fields, recording latency, size and the share with a DOI and a publication venue.
- **Dependencies.** None for the spike. AFF-H uses the author ids.
- **Verification.** Not reviewed: ideas were not put to verification. A completeness reviewer placed it at P2. It stays P1 here because it is the cheapest route to DOIs, published venues and author ids, but the spike comes first.

## 7. Citation strategy

### 7.1 Assessment

- **What is right.**
  - Semantic Scholar is the right count source for CS preprints: it counts all versions, and OpenAlex undercounts by ×1.7 in median, as measured in the maintainer's own experiment.
  - Citations per year of age with `max(age, 1)` is a fair, bounded score.
  - The 22,000 budget and 1.15 hysteresis behave as designed: only 73 archived papers sit between rank 22,000 and 25,300.
  - Counts live in one sorted sidecar file, which keeps the weekly churn to about 97 KB.
- **What is wrong.** The problems are all in **failure handling**. When S2 misbehaves, the archive and the counts can degrade silently. The web cannot distinguish "unknown" from "0", and it does not say how old the counts are.

### 7.2 Items

#### CIT-1 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: Below the 95% gate, the archive run still overwrites `citations.json`, and an S2 outage can empty it

- **Problem.** When coverage < `MIN_COVERAGE`, `update_archive` freezes the selection but still calls `store.write_citations` (`update.py:174-179`). That is a full replacement dated `now()`, holding only the counts fetched before S2 stopped. The 7-day counts cache has always expired by the next Sunday, so a run refused from its first batch writes an empty file. The run exits 0, and the deploy publishes it.
- **Evidence.** An offline reproduction with S2 refusing from the 21st of 40 ids gave coverage 0.50 and an unchanged archive, but `citations.json` went from 40 to 20 counts, dated today. Refusing every batch took a 4-count file to 0. The existing test asserts only the archive.
- **Why it matters.** A bad S2 Sunday removes between 5% and 100% of the counts. Highly cited, "Most cited" and "Size by citations" degrade, while the page claims "as of today". After an outage, the next weekday's `citations` step refills the file, since it asks again for every paper without a count, so the site shows blank counts for about a day and Highly cited falls back to newest-first. With an invalid or revoked key, the file stays empty until someone notices. The daily step then keeps the wrong date through `merge_citations`.
- **Change.** Under the gate, merge the new counts into the existing file (`merge_citations`, which keeps the old date) instead of replacing it, mirroring the daily path. Assert it in `test_the_archive_stays_as_it_is_without_citation_counts`.
- **Benefit.** A failed week leaves the published counts as they were, with an honest date.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** None: merging only adds newer counts.
- **Dependencies.** CIT-4.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (5 votes of 6). The reviewers sharpened "partial" into "possibly empty", and credited the daily step's self-repair after an outage.

#### CIT-2 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: Above the gate, an archived paper whose count is missing this week leaves the archive, bypassing the hysteresis

- **Problem.** `fetch_citation_counts` stops at the first dead batch, so the missing ids are a contiguous tail of up to 5% of 207,000 papers (about 10,000). `select_archive` drops candidates with a falsy count (`selection.py:83`), so archived papers in that tail, or those S2 answers `null` this week, rank nowhere and leave. The tail holds only about 37 archived papers today, because the catalog appends new entries last. The larger path is S2's `null`: a `null` count is present in `counts`, so the 95% check passes (99.99%), while numeric coverage is 99.51%, with 541 `null` among 168,488 old candidates. Graduated papers lose their HTML-derived affiliations: if they come back, it is through the catalog and OpenAlex.
- **Evidence.**
  - A reproduction with the last id refused gave coverage 0.975, and archived paper 2001.00039 left.
  - S2 nulls do flap. Three archived papers have counts in `citations.json` but `null` in the same day's cache. One is 1803.10417, with 3,765 citations.
- **Change.** Before ranking, fill missing or `null` counts of currently archived ids from last week's `citations.json`, and count the carried-over values in the report.
- **Benefit.** The archive stops churning for reasons unrelated to citations, and no enrichment is lost.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A paper genuinely removed from S2 stays until its old count falls out of the margin. That is acceptable.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** A tie (3 votes for P1, 3 for P2), so it stays **P1**.

#### CIT-3 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: With `--prune`, papers whose catalog date is later than their day file are deleted with it

- **Problem.** Stale days are chosen by the day-file date, but graduating papers are candidates only if `not is_recent(first_seen)`, with `first_seen` from the catalog. For 180 of 3,818 day-file papers (4.7%), `first_seen` is 1–27 days after `announced`. That is the worst case: 77 of them (2.0%) are lost whatever the weekday of the prune run. For example, 2609.00036 was announced on 2026-09-02, with `first_seen` 2026-09-14. On a prune run such a paper is "recent", so it is not kept, and its day file is deleted. It vanishes for up to about 33 days (the gap, plus up to 6 days until the next weekly run), drops out of `citations.json` meanwhile, and comes back without its HTML affiliations, author mapping and ROR names.
- **Why it matters.** Prune is off today, but the 12-month design depends on turning it on. The loss is silent and hits the best-enriched papers.
- **Change.** For stored papers, use `first_seen = min(paper.announced, entry.first_seen)`, and add a test with a catalog entry created after its day-file date.
- **Benefit.** Graduation becomes exact, and no enriched paper is lost.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** None.
- **Dependencies.** Must land before prune is enabled (§21).
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 2 for P2): pruning is off, so nothing is lost today. It must still land before pruning is turned on.

#### CIT-4 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: No alarm when the gate trips, when counts go stale, or when the S2 key is invalid

- **Problem.**
  - The gate only logs a warning, and `_run_archive` returns 0.
  - A gated run still rewrites `citations.json`, with a fresh date over fewer counts (CIT-1). The page's "as of" date then looks current while the archive selection is weeks old.
  - An invalid or revoked key gets 403 on every request, yet `_fetch_batch` retries it 30 times, about 25 minutes, and the command exits 0. The last warning does print the 403, once, after those 25 minutes.
  - The web shows "as of <date>" in a tooltip, with no age warning.
- **Why it matters.** A single maintainer relies on green runs. A months-old archive behind a green badge is a silent failure.
- **Change.**
  - Give `FetchError` a `status`. Stop at the first 401/403 with "Semantic Scholar rejected the API key".
  - When coverage < 95%, emit a `::warning::` annotation and a step-summary line, or a non-zero exit after the safe commit (decision in §21).
  - `stats` and the web flag counts older than 14 days as stale.
  - Add `timeout-minutes: 15` to the daily citations step.
- **Benefit.** Failures become visible within a day or a week.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Red runs during S2 outages. `archive.yml`'s *Commit data* has no `if: always()` (only `update.yml`'s has), so add it before the gate exits non-zero, or the safe commit and the deploy are skipped.
- **Dependencies.** CI-2.
- **Verification.** A tie (3 votes for P1, 3 for P2), so it stays **P1**.

#### CIT-11 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: With pruning off (the default), papers stored in day files never compete for the archive

- **Problem.** Selection and deletion are coupled. With `--prune` off (`archive.yml:34`), `stale_days` is empty (`update.py:113`), so every day-file paper, whatever its age, is in `recent_ids` and excluded from the candidates (`update.py:114-116, 133-134`), and graduation never runs (`update.py:140`). The test suite pins this as intended: `test_update_archive_without_pruning_keeps_old_days_out_of_the_archive` archives a 1-citation paper while a 500-citation day-file paper stays out (`test_archive_update.py:168-178`).
- **Evidence.** Offline runs with the repository's fakes, with a day file 400 days old and prune off: the 500-citation paper is not archived, and the 1-citation one is. Today no day file is older than 12 months, so nothing is lost yet.
- **Why it matters.**
  - From September 2027, the best papers of each new year never reach Highly cited, the archive-year graph or Rankings, and the freed budget goes to weaker catalog papers.
  - It starts much sooner if the paused backfill resumes. 2025-10-01 crosses 12 months today, so every backfilled day from October 2025 would be kept out at once. A backfill reaching September 2025 would evict the 162 papers of that month already in `archive/2025.json`.
  - The docstrings (`update.py:3-13`, `config.py:36-37`) describe a rolling year that the default never enforces.
- **Change.** Make `first_seen` age the only recency gate, and let prune decide only what is deleted:
  - Always compute `stale_days`, and build `graduating` from them.
  - Pass `stale_days if prune else []` to `store.remove_days`.
  - Write counts for every id still on disk: the kept day files plus the archive.
  - Rewrite the pinned test to assert the opposite, and add one for an old paper cross-listed into a recent day file (CIT-12).
  - Reword the docstrings and CLAUDE.md to say what prune changes and what it does not (DOC-15).
- **Benefit.** The archive grows correctly whether or not the maintainer prunes, and pruning becomes a pure storage decision (§21).
- **Risks and trade-offs.** An old paper can then be in both a day file and an archive year. No view loads both (`use-papers.ts:54-60`), and `PaperIndex` dedupes by id. The change reverses a deliberate, tested choice (538cbb1 made pruning opt-in as a safeguard), so it needs your agreement.
- **Dependencies.** It must land before any backfill (PROD-1). Same code region as CIT-3 and CIT-12.
- **Verification.** Found by a completeness review: 2 votes for P1 and 1 for P2, so **P1**. The reviewers narrowed the title, since what keeps a paper out is being in a day file, not its announcement date. They also noted that turning prune on later graduates the backlog in one run, with its enrichment.

## 8. Author and affiliation strategy

### 8.1 Assessment

**Coverage.** Affiliations are the product's main differentiator, and coverage is good for recent papers: 80% of tried papers have them, 92% of those have an author mapping, and 85% have named institutions. The extraction design is careful: tiered, conservative, and backed by real fixtures. Its weaknesses sit in four places:

1. **Noise that passes the cleaner.** Person names, junk labels, obfuscated e-mails and glued or split strings. The problem is not that the heuristics are bad, but that there is **no corpus-level quality metric**: each fix is driven by one observed page.
2. **The lifecycle.** Fixes never reach stored data (DATA-1), and revisions misalign mappings (DATA-2).
3. **The archive.** OpenAlex's empty answers are final, so the recent archive years are 48% and 16% affiliated.
4. **Entity resolution.** Institutions are merged by name, in two languages. Authors are merged by name, with no disambiguation.

**Has the heuristic approach reached its ceiling?** Not yet. The measurable remaining gaps are specific:
- LaTeX classes with no fixture: BMVC papers are 90% empty and IROS 36%.
- About 20 recurring noise patterns, listed below.
- Unit lines split from their institution: 193 cases.

Alternatives like OpenAlex cannot replace HTML for recent papers. Their coverage of arXiv-DOI works is 6–10% for papers 12–15 months old. They are useful as a delayed complement, not as a primary source. A learned filter is plausible later (AFF-L), once a quality corpus exists.

### 8.2 Items

#### AFF-1 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Author names and lists of author names are stored as affiliations, and mapped onto authors

- **Problem.** The only guard is an exact `casefold()` comparison of the whole string with one author name (`arxiv_html.py:163-164`). The module's own `_fold` (accent and punctuation folding) is not used there. Several cases pass:
  - An accented variant: "Nicolás Ugrinovic" against the listing's "Nicolas Ugrinovic".
  - A trailing period: "Eduil Nascimento Jr" against "Jr.".
  - A repeated names line: "Yiqun Zhang, and Yiu-Ming Cheung".
  - IEEE grades: "Benoît Macq Fellow, IEEE, and Christophe De Vleeschouwer Member, IEEE".
- **Evidence.**
  - About 70 strings in 63 papers consist only of their own authors' names (the reviews counted 70 to 72), and nearly all are mapped onto an author.
  - A web-side guard (`graph.ts:121-127`, since df2661f) drops an institution named after one of the paper's authors. So only 2 reach the graph and Rankings: "Benoît Macq Fellow" and "Shuai Tao & Wulong Liu". The rest show on paper cards, in the affiliation filter, in the search index and in the published data, with wrong author pairs.
  - It is visible on the very first card of the default view (2609.38180), and as the graph label "Imperial College London. Ivan Kapelyukh…" (live walkthrough).
- **Change.**
  - Compare with a key that folds accents and punctuation, like the web's `searchKey`.
  - Also drop a candidate when every segment, split on `,`, `;`, "and" and "&" after removing IEEE grades and "Jr." suffixes, folds to one of *this paper's* authors.
  - Add a fixture for the IEEE two-line author block (2608.29289 or 2608.30420).
  - Filter inside `_affiliations`, before the tier fallback. Filtering after it would leave 44 of the 63 papers with `[]` (final), and would skip the thanks tier, where IEEE-style papers often state the real affiliations.
  - Keep a string that ROR names or that has an institution word. 2609.25650 lists the consortium "Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative" as both an author and an affiliation.
  - When dropping a name leaves one affiliation, do not let the single-affiliation shortcut give it to every author: 2609.38180 would then map all six authors to Carnegie Mellon.
  - Apply the fix to stored data through `reclean` (DATA-1).
- **Benefit.** About 70 wrong strings and their wrong author pairs disappear from cards, filters and data. The web guard can stay as a backstop.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** An institution named exactly like one of the paper's own authors would be lost. That already happens once (the consortium above), so keep the exception.
- **Dependencies.** DATA-1 for stored data.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 13 for P2, 1 for P3, over five reviews). The main correction is the graph impact above: the web already hides almost all of these names.

#### AFF-2 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Obfuscated e-mails are published as affiliations, against the data contract

- **Problem.** `_NOISE_RE` rejects a segment only for `@`, `http(s)://` or `www.`. It misses `(at)` and `[at]` obfuscations, and brace lists of local parts whose `@domain` landed in another segment.
- **Evidence.** 4 records:
  - 2609.12885: a personal mailbox written with `(at)`.
  - 2609.37046: a lab mailbox written with `[at]`, glued to a real affiliation (`… Munich, Germany <mailbox> [at] <domain>`).
  - 2609.05518: `Jilin University` followed by a brace list of mailbox names (`{<name>, <name>, …}`).
  - 2609.12771: a bare brace list of mailbox names.
  - (The addresses are not reproduced in this report.)
- **Why it matters.** The contract says affiliations "never contain e-mails or URLs". The site republishes and indexes them. They are the authors' own addresses, published on arXiv in the same form, and 4 of 7,744 strings, but the contract promises none.
- **Change.**
  - Extend the noise rules with `\s[\[(]\s*at\s*[\])]\s` and brace lists of identifiers.
  - Strip the trailing address instead of dropping the whole segment.
  - Add 2609.37046 and 2609.05518 as fixtures.
  - Make "no contact details" a structural rule of `validate` (DATA-3).
- **Benefit.** The privacy claim becomes true and enforced.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A too-broad `{` rule could drop LaTeX leftovers that are real affiliations. Test it on all 23 fixtures.
- **Dependencies.** DATA-1 (reclean) repairs the stored records.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (2 votes for P1, 4 for P2). Strip the brace lists, do not reject them: "Jilin University{…}" holds a real affiliation that ROR names.

#### AFF-3 · P1 · Verified · Medium · M: OpenAlex's early "nothing" is stored as final, so the recent archive years are mostly unaffiliated

- **Problem.** `fetch_affiliations` returns an empty tuple both for works OpenAlex does not know (a 404) and for known works with no institutions yet. `_with_affiliations` refills only `affiliations is None` (`update.py:263`), so every `[]` is frozen at the week the paper entered. OpenAlex coverage grows with age for recent papers. By month of first version: 50–70% in 2023, 38–57% in 2024, then 39%, 20%, **0%**, 23%, 26%, 10%, 10%, 7% and 6% from January to September 2025.
- **Evidence.**
  - 10,450 of 22,071 archive papers (47%) are `[]`, and 0 are `null`.
  - 2025 has 304 affiliated papers of 1,921 (16%).
  - Rankings for 2025 rest on those 304 papers, which the UI does state (live walkthrough).
- **Why it matters.** The Rankings and the graph of recent archive years, and the "which labs wrote the most cited papers of 2025" story, are built on a sixth of the papers. The 35,030 papers of the current gap will enter the same way.
- **Change.**
  - *Option A (weekly, cheap).*
    - Return `None` instead of `[]` for "not found" or "no institutions" while the paper is under about 24 months old.
    - Store the lookup date (META-3), and re-ask `[]` papers older than 30 days since their last lookup.
    - That is about 2,150 papers under 24 months, about 45 requests. Only 2024 (52% empty) and 2025 (84%) are above OpenAlex's steady 40–47% empty rate of 2015–2023, so retries can recover at most about 1,100 papers, not 10,450.
  - *Option B (one-off, about 14 h).* Archive papers with ids ≥ 2312 have HTML renderings, so run them through the existing front-matter path once.
  - Fix the misleading comment at `update.py:171`.
- **Benefit.** 2024–2025 affiliation coverage rises from 48% and 16% towards the 60% of older years (A), or towards recent-data levels (B).
- **Risks and trade-offs.**
  - A changes the meaning of `[]` for the archive ("retry while young"), which must be documented in the contract. Storing 404 as final was deliberate and is tested (9d13c27, `test_openalex.py:52`).
  - B mixes HTML strings with web-side naming into an archive whose institutions are all ROR names today.
  - The 0% for 2025-03 may be an OpenAlex data gap rather than a lag. **Spike first:** 20 ids of 2503.* and 20 of 2409.*, counting authorships with institutions.
- **Dependencies.** META-3 (lookup date). The choice between A and B is in §21.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (4 votes of 6).

#### AFF-4 · P3 · Idea · Quick · S: Option: in the co-authorship projection, link an unplaced author only to the institutions no co-author holds

- **Problem.** In the projection (papers off), an author the paper does not place is linked to all of its institutions (`graph.ts:233-235`), while papers-on mode refuses those edges. This is a documented design, not a bug. Commit 22904e5 says "unplaced authors keep an edge to every institution of the paper, as before". The docstring says so (`graph.ts:90-93`), `graph.test.ts:171-199` asserts it, and CLAUDE.md defines the projection as nodes "linked when they share a paper".
- **Evidence.** 420 of 2,890 affiliated recent papers are partly mapped, with 1,432 unplaced authors. They give 5,208 of the 22,939 author–institution edges (23%), and 4,828 of those go to an institution the paper gives to another author.
- **Change.** If the projection should mean "works at" rather than "co-signed with", link an unplaced author of a partly mapped paper only to the institutions that no placed co-author holds, and keep all of them for wholly unmapped papers. That removes most of the 4,828 edges and keeps the likely-true ones: in 86 of the 420 papers, an affiliation belongs to no placed author. The tests at `graph.test.ts:171-199` change with it.
- **Benefit.** Author–institution edges then mean affiliation in both modes.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** It changes a deliberate choice. The edges it removes are true as co-occurrence, so "N shared papers" stays literally right today.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Refuted as a defect by four of six reviewers: the behavior is documented, intended and tested. It is kept as a **P3 idea**.

## 9. Search and discovery

### 9.1 Assessment

- **What is good.**
  - Search is fast (0.1–2.5 ms per query on 3.8k papers), accent-insensitive everywhere, and indexes derived institution names, so "CMU" finds "Carnegie Mellon University".
  - It highlights the terms MiniSearch actually matched and dedupes cross-lists.
  - Relevance is explained.
- **What is wrong.** The problems are **precision and consistency**. Clicked names filter by substring. Short acronyms expand into names. Phrases and exclusions use a different text and a different tokenizer than the free-text search. The Back button leaves the site.
- **Why it matters.** For a literature-review tool, precision on names and institutions is the core drill-down: "who works on this, and what else did they publish".

### 9.2 Items

#### SRCH-1 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: Author and affiliation filters match substrings, so a clicked name returns other people and institutions

- **Problem.** `containsFolded` (`search.ts:184-188`) keeps a paper when any author or affiliation *contains* the folded filter text. Clicking an author on a card, a Rankings row or a graph node sets an exact name, but the match stays a substring.
- **Evidence.**
  - Author filter "Hao Li" returns 68 papers, while no paper has an author "Hao Li" (it matches Chuanhao Li, Yuhao Liu…). "Yi Li" returns 32 vs 0, and "Wei Wang" 37 vs 16.
  - Affiliation "Intel" returns 370 papers, 367 of them "…Intelligence…". "University of Hong Kong" returns 123, and 77 of those are CUHK, CUHK-Shenzhen or CityU.
  - For 60 of the top 100 institutions and 53 of the top 100 authors in Rankings, the row count disagrees with the list that "Show their papers" opens. A row reads "Hao Wang, 11 papers" and opens 51.
  - Over a set of 21 filter queries, mean precision is 0.36 for author filters ("Wei Li": 48 results for 10 right ones; "Yu Li": 84 for 7) and 0.60 for affiliation filters, at recall 1. An independent recount over the 3,816 papers gives the same author numbers.
- **Why it matters.** The drill-down silently returns 3–8 times the right set, and the Rankings and graph numbers look wrong next to the lists they open.
- **Change.**
  - Clicked names match exactly on `searchKey`, or on `paperInstitutions` for institutions, as the graph's node key does.
  - Typed text matches word prefixes of `searchKey`, not substrings, so "kaist" still finds "KAIST, Daejeon" and "nvid" still finds NVIDIA while typing. Substring matching was intentional: the docstring (`search.ts:190-195`) and `search.test.ts:92-99` pin it, and the filter runs on every keystroke.
  - Word boundaries alone do not fix nested institution names ("University of Hong Kong" is a whole-word part of "The Chinese University of Hong Kong"), so clicked institutions must compare institution keys.
  - Encode the mode in the URL (`author=` typed vs `author_id=`/`inst=` exact), keeping old links on word-boundary semantics.
  - Add a test asserting that `rankEntities` counts equal the filtered list length.
- **Benefit.** A clicked entity shows exactly its papers, and Rankings, graph and list agree.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Exact mode loses variants ("K. He" vs "Kaiming He"), which word-boundary typing keeps.
- **Dependencies.** None. AFF-14 and AFF-H later make the keys stronger.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1**, unanimously (6 votes).

#### SRCH-2 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Phrases and exclusions ignore authors, affiliations and venue, and disagree with the tokenizer

- **Problem.** `searchableText` is only title + abstract + comments (`search.ts:109-111`). Phrases are raw substrings, and exclusions are members of a word set split on non-alphanumerics.
- **Evidence.**
  - Quoting an author returns nothing ("Yu-Rou Tuan" unquoted: 2, quoted: 0). `"Carnegie Mellon University"` returns 0.
  - `-google` keeps all 19 Google-affiliated papers.
  - `"gan"` matches inside "organ" (234 of 259 hits).
  - `"self supervised"` finds 1 paper and `"self-supervised"` 129, while free text treats them alike (166 each). Measured on a query set and recounted independently: `"zero shot"` finds 1 of the 237 papers that write zero-shot or zero shot, `"open vocabulary"` 3 of 61, and `"self supervised learning"` 0 of 22. Typing the hyphen works ("zero-shot" finds 238 of 238): only the spaced spelling fails.
  - `-self-supervised` excludes nothing, and `-survey` keeps "surveys".
  - The hint and the README promise phrases "over titles, abstracts, authors, affiliations".
- **Change.** Build the constraint text once per paper from every searched field (authors, affiliations, institutions, venue included), normalized with `searchKey` and padded with spaces. Phrases then match on word boundaries and ignore hyphens. Exclusions compare `searchKey` token sequences, optionally as prefixes to mirror the positive side. Precompute the text in the `PaperIndex` constructor: this also removes a 134 ms per-search cost. Phrase highlights must follow, since `highlight.ts` matches needles only at word starts: a normalized phrase would match "self-supervised" and highlight nothing.
- **Benefit.** The documented syntax becomes true, and exclusions become a real de-noising tool (`-survey -medical -google`).
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Exclusions become broader: an excluded word in an affiliation now drops the paper, which is the documented intent.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (4 votes for P1, 5 for P2): zero results are visible, and the sidebar filters cover names. The silent-wrong case is an excluded author or affiliation word (`-wang` keeps 837 of the 842 papers with a Wang author). Ship it with SRCH-1, since both rebuild the same constraint text.

#### SRCH-3 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: Prefix on every term and fuzzy from 5 characters flood acronym and name queries with false matches

- **Problem.** `prefix: true` applies to every term, including finished short acronyms. Fuzzy is a 0.2 ratio: distance 1 from 5 characters, and 2 from 8 ("detection" matches "selection"). Author names are boosted ×2.
- **Evidence (live walkthrough plus a Node probe).**
  - "GAN" returns 34 papers, and **its top 8 by relevance are papers by authors named Gan**. The marked words are Gan, Gandolfi, Gandhi, Gangireddy.
  - "ViT" matches Vittorio, Vito, Viterbi and "vitalizing", and its top result is not a ViT paper.
  - "depth" highlights "death".
  - "Wei Wang" as free text gives 257 results, only 16 with that author, and 2 in the top 10.
  - "MIT" as free text returns 266 papers at precision 0.04 (mitigate, mitigation…).
  - Over 32 topical queries, the current options return 4,767 results at precision 0.67. Last-term prefix with 1-edit fuzzy from 7 characters returns 4,213 at 0.72, with the same recall.
- **Why it matters.** The relevance order keeps exact hits on top for most single words. The damage shows in the orders that ignore the score: newest, citations, and the Highly cited view, which always ranks by citations. There, "sam" puts 10 non-SAM papers in the top 10 of an archive year. Every overmatch is also highlighted as a match.
- **Change.** `prefix: (term, i, terms) => i === terms.length - 1 && term.length >= 4` gives type-ahead on the last word only, and never on a short acronym typed alone ("MIT" stops matching "mitigate"). Use an absolute fuzzy distance, `fuzzy: (term) => (term.length >= 7 ? 1 : false)`: a higher ratio threshold barely helps. Keep `weights: {fuzzy: 0.2, prefix: 0.5}` so exact hits dominate. Highlight only exact or prefix-of-typed terms. For names, add an `author:` field prefix (SRCH-6).
- **Benefit.** CV acronyms (ViT, GAN, SAM, DiT, CLIP) behave like keywords, and absurd highlights disappear.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Some plural and typo recall is lost. Re-tune the "tolerates typos" test.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (4 votes of 6). The reviewers showed that the first proposal (prefix on the last term *or* on 4+ characters) kept prefix on a lone acronym. The change above is the corrected, measured one.

#### SRCH-4 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: The Back button never returns to a previous search or view

- **Problem.** `useUrlState` writes every change with `history.replaceState`, and nothing listens to `popstate`. The tabs are buttons, not links. Papers → Graph → Back therefore leaves the site, and middle-click on a tab does nothing.
- **Change.** Use `pushState` for navigational patches: view, paper, year, period, and filters set by clicks. Keep `replaceState` for typing and sort. Add a `popstate` listener that re-reads the state, guarded so that the re-read does not push again (that would drop the forward stack). Render the tabs as `<a href={writeState(…)} aria-current>`.
- **Benefit.** Exploratory drill-downs become undoable, and tabs open in new tabs.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Too many history entries if typing pushes, so keep typing on replace.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (5 votes of 6). Back leaves the site without losing state, since Forward restores it. The real cost is that drill-downs cannot be undone: the graph's Open paper and Show papers reset the query and the filters (`app.tsx:245-264`).

#### SRCH-5 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: Share links from the Highly cited view open an empty day

- **Problem.** `linkTo` (`app.tsx:109-115`) always builds the default view plus the paper's announcement day. For an archived paper (announced in 2017, say), no day file exists. The link loads zero papers, with a notice that the paper "may be older than the papers kept" and a link to arXiv. That is misleading here, since the paper is kept, in the archive. Only the copied link is wrong: a plain click leaves a working `?view=cited&year=…&paper=…` URL in the address bar. Highly cited is where people share classics.
- **Change.** When the paper is older than the oldest stored day and its year is an archive year, emit `{view: "cited", year, paper}`, as `showInGraph` already does. Add a test.
- **Benefit.** Every Share link resolves to the paper.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** None notable.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (4 votes of 6). All 22,071 archived papers are affected.

## 10. UX/UI and accessibility

### 10.1 Assessment

- **Visual design.** The interface is clean, fast and consistent. It is typographically careful (a serif for titles, restrained color), with a coherent theme system (auto, light and dark, restored before first paint). There is no horizontal page overflow at 375 px.
- **Semantics.** The semantic baseline is above average (§3).
- **Gaps.** The remaining work falls into five groups:
  1. **Contrast failures** in secondary text and links.
  2. **The graph**, which is mostly mouse-only and cannot zoom on touch.
  3. **State that is not exposed**: the active tab, history, the page title.
  4. **Mobile ergonomics**: hidden tabs, a large sticky block, hidden filters.
  5. **Onboarding and empty or error states.**

Live observations (desktop, 375×812 phone, dark mode):
- On a phone, the tab strip overflows inside its pill (scroll width 459 px vs 303 px visible). "Rankings" is cut and "Reading list" is off-screen, with no scroll cue.
- The sticky search block is 156 px tall on a 812 px phone screen (19%), because the syntax hint is inside it.
- The graph renders and pans on mobile, but cannot zoom, and long labels overflow the right edge.
- Dark mode renders the list and the graph correctly.
- The focus ring is visible (the UA outline).

### 10.2 Items

#### UX-1 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Several text and link pairs fail WCAG AA contrast, in the places users read most

- **Problem and evidence.** Measured over the actual CSS tokens:
  - In-text links in abstracts and comments are distinguished by color alone (`a { text-decoration: none }`). Links differ from abstract text by 3.01:1 (light) and **1.93:1 (dark)**, and from comment text by **1.06:1 / 1.21:1**. WCAG 1.4.1 requires 3:1 or a non-color cue, and comments are where code and project URLs live.
  - Highlighted matches in affiliations and comments in dark mode reach 3.02:1 (`mark { color: inherit }` on muted text), and 3.65:1 in the venue badge.
  - In the calendar, in-range day numbers reach 4.12:1 (light), and the 9.6 px paper counts on busy days 3.87:1 and 4.00:1.
  - Form field borders reach 1.29:1, and the saved star (hard-coded `#e8a400`, the only color outside the tokens) 2.01:1 on light.
- **Change.**
  - Underline in-text links: `.paper__abstract a, .paper__comments a { text-decoration: underline; text-underline-offset: 2px; overflow-wrap: anywhere }`.
  - Use `mark { color: var(--text) }`.
  - Render calendar in-range text in `var(--text)` with weight 600, and counts in `var(--text)` at 0.65 rem.
  - Add a `--border-strong` token (#8a8a84 / #6a6a66) for inputs, and a `--star` token (#b27b00 light, #e8a400 dark) in all three palette blocks.
- **Benefit.** Every reading context passes AA in both themes. The fixes measure 12.41:1, 12.32:1, 3.47:1 and 3.67:1.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A slightly busier abstract, and a less blue range selection. The range edges stay solid accent.
- **Dependencies.** Keep both dark blocks identical (the CLAUDE.md rule, which a test should guard: TEST-9).
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 8 for P2): link text is a literal URL, which signals a link without color, and the near misses are dark-mode only.

#### UX-2 · P2 · Verified · Medium · M: The graph cannot be zoomed on touch and is mostly unreachable by keyboard

- **Problem.**
  - The canvas sets `touch-action: none` and handles single pointers plus the wheel, with no pinch and no zoom buttons. On a phone or tablet the default 5,400-node graph can be panned and tapped, and zoom only jumps: Fit, or focusing a node (at least 1.5×) from the details panel, the Highlight field or a `?paper=` link. Browser pinch is blocked too.
  - The canvas is not focusable. Keyboard users reach the graph through each card's Graph button and the Highlight field (Tab or `/`), whose Enter selects the *first* match only.
  - The canvas is `role="img"` with a fixed label, so a screen reader learns nothing of its contents.
- **Change.**
  - *Touch.* Track pointers in a `Map`. With two pointers, apply the same zoom-about-point math as the wheel, extracted as a pure, tested `zoomAbout`. Add + and − buttons next to Fit, which also serve keyboards. Use a touch-specific hint.
  - *Keyboard and screen reader.*
    - Enter and Shift+Enter cycle through matches, with "k of n matches" in a `role=status`.
    - Focus moves to the details panel after a keyboard selection.
    - A listbox shows the top 10 matches under the Highlight field.
    - The canvas gets a dynamic label ("2,828 papers, 2,559 institutions, 6,889 links") and an `aria-describedby` pointing to Rankings as the text equivalent.
- **Benefit.** The graph becomes usable on phones and tablets, with a complete, discoverable keyboard path.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A pinch conflicts with node drag, so start the pinch only when the second pointer lands. Focus should move only on keyboard selection.
- **Dependencies.** None. The accessibility target is a decision (§21): a full path, or "visual exploration, with Rankings as the text equivalent".
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (4 votes for P1, 5 for P2): Rankings is a keyboard-accessible table of the same entities, and Fit and focus give two zoom jumps on touch. Continuous zoom and match cycling are what is missing.

#### UX-4 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: The sticky search block hides the focused element, even at 100% zoom

- **Problem.** The sticky search block includes the query-syntax hint. It is 97 px tall at 1280×800, 117 px on a landscape phone, and 156 px at 400% zoom, which is 61% of the viewport. The browser does not scroll a focused element out from under it, so keyboard focus lands behind the bar (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured). On desktop it also overlaps the top of the sticky sidebar (`top: 76px`).
- **Evidence.** Measured live: a control focused while scrolling up sat 38–58 px from the top, entirely under the bar. A reviewer measured that at 1280×800 the bar fully hides 11 of 60 backward focus stops (about 1 in 6), and partly hides 7 more.
- **Why it matters.** Keyboard users lose track of where they are on every backward move, at the default zoom. At high zoom, the search block takes most of the screen.
- **Change.** Move the hint out of the sticky block (a "?" toggle, or a line below the results count). Add `html { scroll-padding-top: <bar height> }` and `@media (max-height: 500px) { .search { position: static } }`.
- **Benefit.** Focus is always visible, and phones and zoomed views get their screen back.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** The hint becomes one click away, so keep it visible on a first visit.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Raised from P2 to **P1** by the votes (2 for P1, 1 for P2). The reviewers corrected the 400% figure (156 px, not about 170) and showed the failure at 100% zoom.

### 11.2 Items

#### PERF-1 · P1 · Verified · Architectural · M: "All days" and wide ranges download and index everything, and will not survive a year of data

- **Problem.** `RANGES.all` is `Infinity` (`period.ts:8`), and custom ranges are unbounded. `usePapers` fetches every selected day, then builds one MiniSearch index over all of them on the main thread (`search.ts:125-142`). At one year that is about 117 MB of JSON (about 33 MB gzip), 20 s of indexing and about 480 MB of heap (the index alone about 340 MB). That is one click away in the period picker, and it will crash phones. Visits that pick it are also the main Pages bandwidth cost. Without pruning (the default), day files are not even capped at a year (CIT-11, ARCH-1).
- **Why it matters.** It is the scaling cliff of the architecture, and it gates PROD-1 (filling the 11-month gap).
- **Change.**
  1. *Now (S).* Replace "All days" with a bounded preset (e.g. "Last 60 days"). Cap custom ranges to a paper budget (e.g. 10,000, using the counts already in `index.json`), with a message. The picker already shows the paper count, so add per-day byte sizes to `index.json` and warn before loading.
  2. *Next (S).* Build with `addAllAsync(papers, {chunkSize: 200})` so the UI stays responsive. Show the list before the index finishes. Cache loaded day files in memory across period changes, and abort in-flight fetches.
  3. *Then (M), before the backfill resumes.*
     - Publish a light **per-month search shard** at build time: id, title, authors, institutions, venue and date, at about 146 B per paper gzip vs 736 B with abstracts, so about 6.6 MB for a year.
     - Year-wide searches then run over titles, authors and institutions, and full day files load only for the days displayed.
     - Abstract search stays available for the selected days, and the UI says so.
  4. *Only if needed (M).* Move MiniSearch to a Web Worker. A prebuilt serialized index is not worth it: 1.79 MB gzip extra per 3.8k papers, and it saves only about 0.9 s.
- **Benefit.** Predictable memory and load time regardless of how much history is stored, and a year of titles and authors becomes searchable.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Year-wide abstract search is lost client-side, so make the scope explicit. `?range=all` links need a redirect to the cap.
- **Dependencies.** The retention decision (§21). Shards need a small pipeline or build step next to `write_index`.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (7 votes of 9).

#### PERF-2 · P2 · Verified · Medium · M: Turning on the graph's author layer quadruples the graph and freezes the main thread for about 8 s

- **Problem.** `maxNodes: 0` ("show everything") with `minAuthorPapers: 1` means that ticking "Authors" jumps from 5.4k to 23.6k nodes on "All days", most of them one-paper authors. At the default 5-day period it gives 8,567 nodes, which settle in about 2.5 s. The d3 simulation runs on the main thread with a full redraw per tick: about 97 ms plus 33 ms per frame, so about 8 fps for 83 ticks. Every drag reheats it. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, the layout runs synchronously on every change: about 5.8 s of frozen UI with three layers.
- **Change.**
  - *Cheap first (S).* When "Authors" is turned on, raise `minAuthorPapers` to 2 automatically (8,948 nodes instead of 23,639) and say so in the note. Keep "everything" for the default layers.
  - While `alpha > 0.1` on large graphs, draw every second tick.
  - Under reduced motion, have `reheat` settle synchronously too, and hide the dead Reheat button.
  - Cap replay reheats, and bucket replay by week for archive years (341 days in 2024 means 5 minutes that never settle).
  - *Then (L).* Move the simulation into a Web Worker. It exchanges positions as a transferable `Float32Array`, while drawing and hit-testing stay on the main thread. d3-force already runs headless in Node without changes.
- **Benefit.** The author layer becomes usable, and input stays responsive while layouts settle.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Changing a stored default changes the graph for returning users (bump the preferences key). A Worker adds one message round-trip to drag latency.
- **Dependencies.** PERF-3.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (2 votes for P1, 4 for P2): the 8 fps regime needs a wide period or a large archive year on top of Authors.

#### PERF-3 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: Consortium papers turn archive-year author projections into a 400k-link hairball

- **Problem.** `linkByCoOccurrence` emits every co-author pair, n(n−1)/2 per paper. The 2024 archive holds 2407.21783 (561 authors, 559 distinct, about 156k pairs) and 2410.21276 (420 authors), plus 16 papers with 50 or more authors. The author projection reaches 397k links, 39% of them from one paper, and those edges say nothing about collaboration.
- **Change.** Skip the author clique above `MAX_CLIQUE_AUTHORS` (e.g. 50, a constant with its reason), keeping that paper's author→institution edges. Optionally use Newman's 1/(n−1) weights. Update the CLAUDE.md numbers.
- **Benefit.** The 2024 projection falls from 397k to 109k links (3.6× fewer) and shows real co-authorship.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** The threshold is arbitrary (§21), so say in the UI why such a paper's authors are not linked.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (3 votes of 3): it affects only the opt-in projection with authors on, and Rankings already avoids the clique.

## 12. Architecture, data storage and backend

### 12.1 Data in git: does it scale?

Git as the database is the founding choice (bf42ab6), and it is recorded nowhere. The measurements say it is **fine for one category and the foreseeable future**, with two conditions.

| Quantity | Today | 1 year (cs.CV, prune off) | 1 year × 3 categories |
|---|---|---|---|
| Packed repository (what a clone downloads) | 16.8 MB | about 50–100 MB (≈35 MB of day-file history plus 5–60 MB of weekly archive rewrites: pushes measured 97 KB, packed deltas up to 1.2 MB) | about 150–300 MB |
| Day files in the working tree and the site | 9.4 MB (21 days) | about 110 MB | about 330 MB (plus cross-list duplication) |
| Archive (bounded by budget) | 48 MB | about 50 MB | about 150 MB |
| Deployed site (Pages limit 1 GB) | 58 MB | about 160 MB | about 480 MB |

- **Per-commit cost.** About 730 B of packed history per new paper, and about 97 KB for the weekly archive rewrite (thin pack).
- **Condition 1: retention.** With prune off, day files grow forever. Three categories reach the 1 GB Pages limit in about 2–3 years.
- **Condition 2: bounded loading.** The web must stop loading unbounded ranges (PERF-1).

**Recommendation.**
1. Keep `data/` on `main` for now. It is simple, auditable (`git log data/`) and cheap.
2. Enable `--prune` once 12 months of day files exist, after CIT-3 is fixed. The archive keeps the cited papers, and the rest leave the site but stay in git history.
3. Add a size guard in `deploy.yml` (fail above about 800 MB of `dist/`) and a packed-size check in a scheduled job.
4. **Migration trigger:** a packed `.git` above about 500 MB or a site above about 600 MB, which is realistic only with 3 or more categories. At that point, publish `data/` only through the Pages artifact and keep compact state on an orphan `data` branch squashed monthly. Releases or an external bucket are alternatives.

**Options considered and rejected for now.**
- SQLite or Parquet: the static client would need WASM, and diffs would be lost.
- A real backend: ops cost for one maintainer, and not needed until per-user alerts or sync.

### 13.2 Items

#### REL-1 · P1 · Verified · Medium · M: A change in arXiv's HTML layout would silently mark every new paper "no affiliations", with no retry

- **Problem.** `parse_front_matter` falls back to the whole document when `article.ltx_document` is missing (`arxiv_html.py:192`), and it raises nothing when `div.ltx_authors` is absent (the silent path is `arxiv_html.py:208-214`). After a LaTeXML or arXiv markup change, every page would return `[]`. So would any other 200 page (a maintenance or interstitial page), and a move of the HTML URLs, since a 404 is stored as `[]` (`arxiv_html.py:142-144`). That is final by contract, so it is never retried. The run would exit 0 and the report would say "N affiliations fetched". README says "the network test fails first", but no workflow runs it.
- **Evidence.** The code paths above. The measured baseline for the share of tried papers with `[]` per day is 12–31%.
- **Why it matters.** Weeks of corrupted enrichment before anyone notices, and a custom repair afterwards because `[]` is terminal.
- **Change.**
  1. Add a drift breaker in `_add_html_front_matter`: after 40 or more pages, if 90% or more came back empty (three times the worst measured day), stop, reset this run's empties to `null` (they may already be checkpointed as `[]`, every 20 pages), and raise a `ParseError` ("has arXiv's layout changed?").
  2. Add `empty` to `UpdateReport`.
  3. Add a weekly `canary.yml` running `pytest -m network`, contents read-only, 20 min, with the contact User-Agent. Its failure is the alarm.
- **Benefit.** A layout change becomes a red run the same day, and the papers stay retryable.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A genuine day of scanned papers could trip a tight threshold. The canary may flake on export.arxiv.org throttling, so keep it weekly and drop the API assertion if needed.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (4 votes of 6). A manual recovery exists, and the maintainer has used it (set `affiliations` back to `null`), but the loss stays silent until someone looks.

#### REL-2 · P1 · Verified · Medium · S: A partial arxiv.org outage burns the whole 3.5-hour step: no circuit breaker and no deadline

- **Problem.** `_add_html_front_matter` logs and continues on every `FetchError`, with no consecutive-failure stop (ROR has one) and no deadline. A failing page costs 4 attempts: about 90 s on a fast 503, 310 s when the server hangs. The step is killed at 210 min, which skips ROR and the index (at most 19 pages of results are lost, thanks to the checkpoints). A total outage is caught early, because the listing fails first and the CLI exits 1 within minutes. The exposure is an HTML-only or mid-run failure. Scattered failures matter too: in simulation, 15 timed-out pages out of 600 (2.5%, never two in a row) take the HTML loop to 224 min, and no breaker would trip.
- **Change.** First add `--deadline-minutes` (CI passes about 195), checked before each page, so the pipeline checkpoints, runs ROR, writes the index and prints its report before the kill: it is the part that also covers scattered failures. Then stop after 5 consecutive `FetchError`s (a constant, like `ror.MAX_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES`). Handle SIGTERM and KeyboardInterrupt by checkpointing (REL-6).
- **Benefit.** An outage costs minutes instead of the run, and the run always ends cleanly.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A threshold that is too low stops on a few isolated 5xx, but 5 in a row is already about 25 minutes of failures.
- **Dependencies.** DATA-6.
- **Verification.** Kept at **P1** (3 votes for P1, 2 for P2; one review failed).

#### REL-3 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: No license for the code or the published dataset, and the data terms are unchecked

- **Problem.** There is no `LICENSE` and no `license` field in `pyproject.toml` or `package.json`. Yet the site and 58 MB of data are public on Pages. Without a license, nobody may reuse the code (moot while the repository is private, but the site and data are public). The sources are credited, since the footer and the README name and link all four, but their terms are not stated:
  - arXiv metadata,
  - Semantic Scholar counts (governed by the S2 API license agreement),
  - OpenAlex and ROR (CC0, to confirm).
  - Each paper's own license, which arXiv exposes and the pipeline discards (REL-9).
- **Change.** Choose a code license (MIT or Apache-2.0) and add the SPDX field to both manifests. Add a "Data and licenses" section, in the README and on the site's about page (PROD-7), naming each source's terms. **Read the S2 API license's redistribution and attribution clause** before the repository goes public. If it restricts bulk republication, keep counts in the UI but reconsider the public `citations.json`.
- **Benefit.** Clear reuse rights, and lawful, stated redistribution.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** The choice is the maintainer's (§21). The S2 terms might force a change to `citations.json`.
- **Dependencies.** None.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 5 for P2).

#### SEC-9 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: No way for a person the site ranks to ask for a correction or removal

- **Problem.** The site ranks and graphs named people: Rankings by author, the author layer, and 16,051 author-to-affiliation links in recent data. Its extracted affiliations are sometimes wrong. Neither the site nor the README gives a contact or an issue link, and the repository is private, so its issues are not reachable by the public.
- **Evidence.** The footer (`app.tsx:482-488`) credits sources only, and `git ls-files` shows no about, privacy or contact file. The card of 2609.38180 shows the author name "Nicolás Ugrinovic" as an affiliation, mapped to another author.
- **Why it matters.** For a single maintainer, the cheapest protection against a complaint is a visible channel and a quick, durable fix. Today an affected author has neither, while the site republishes their name, institution and sometimes an e-mail-like string.
- **Change.**
  1. *Now (S).* Add an About section reachable from the top bar. It says what the site is and how affiliations are extracted, and offers "Request a correction or removal" with a contact e-mail (or the repository's issues once public). The same page hosts PROD-7's coverage notes and a privacy note (SEC-10).
  2. *Later (S), if requests come.* Add a versioned `data/overrides.json` (arXiv id → drop the paper, clear its affiliations, or hide an author from Rankings). Apply it in `PaperStore.save` and the archive writer, and check it in `validate` (DATA-3).
- **Benefit.** A complaint becomes a five-minute fix, with a record.

## 15. GitHub Actions and CI/CD

### 15.1 Assessment

The workflows are carefully built.
- Actions are pinned by SHA.
- Checkouts use `ref: main`, so a queued run starts from the latest data.
- A shared concurrency group serializes data runs.
- Deploy goes through `workflow_call` to work around `GITHUB_TOKEN`, with no PAT.
- The catalog lives in the Actions cache, restored by prefix and saved even on failure.
- Inputs are passed through `env:`.

What is missing is **the operational layer around the happy path**: robust commits, validation before publishing, a run summary, alerts, and a canary for upstream drift.

### 15.2 Items

#### CI-1 · P2 · Verified · Quick · S: A data commit that conflicts on rebase throws away the whole run

- **Problem.** *Commit data* runs `git add`, `commit`, `pull --rebase` and `push`, with no conflict handling and no retry (`update.yml:78-85`, and the same in `archive.yml`).
  - `index.json` is rewritten on every run, and line 3 is `generated_at`. So a commit that rewrote the index (a local update, backfill or archive) and reached `main` during the run conflicts every time. `make institutions` and `make citations` do not write it. `citations.json` conflicts differently: concurrent merges both append new ids at the end of the sorted counts.
  - On conflict the step fails, the runner is discarded, and up to 600 HTML pages (about 2.5 h of arXiv crawl) are lost.
  - A plain push race also fails.
- **Evidence.** No run has been lost so far. On 2026-09-30 the collision happened the other way: the maintainer rebased three local data commits (1af82ba, 8dcdefe, cccb374) onto the bot's 7204adc (reflog).
- **Change.** Replace the step with a retrying, derived-file-aware version:
  1. Fetch and rebase onto `origin/main`.
  2. On conflict, take upstream for `citations.json` (tomorrow's run re-fetches the few new counts) and regenerate `index.json` from the files on disk, through a small `preprint-radar index` command.
  3. Abort on any other conflict.
  4. Retry the push 3 times.
  5. On failure, upload `data/` as an artifact kept 7 days.
  6. Expose `pushed=true|false` as a step output (used by CI-3).
  7. Add a working agreement to CLAUDE.md: never push `data/` while *Update data* runs.
- **Benefit.** Concurrent edits and push races stop costing a day of data. A genuine conflict in a day file still fails loudly, with the data kept for recovery.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** A bug in the conflict branch could produce a wrong index. It is regenerated from disk, so it is always consistent with the files.
- **Dependencies.** A pinned `actions/upload-artifact`.
- **Verification.** Lowered to **P2** (1 vote for P1, 5 for P2): the trigger is narrow and has not fired. The fix is still cheap insurance for a 3-hour run.

#### CI-2 · P1 · Verified · Quick · S: Scheduled runs degrade silently: no summary, no alert, no freshness check

- **Problem.**
  - No workflow writes `$GITHUB_STEP_SUMMARY`. Counts, coverage and the newest day exist only as log lines, and the logs have no timestamps (`console.py:16`).
  - No workflow opens an issue or notifies on failure. GitHub's default e-mail for scheduled workflows is easy to miss or filter.
  - Hard failures are not the issue: every `PreprintRadarError` exits 1, fails the job, and GitHub e-mails the maintainer. What ends green is a tripped archive gate, S2 giving up after 30 refusals (an invalid key included), and empty-`[]` drift until REL-1.
  - Nothing checks that the site serves the data just committed.
- **Change.**
  1. **Summary step** (`if: always()`): the newest announcement day and its age, the `stats` table, the per-step counts from an extended `UpdateReport` (abstracts by source, HTML read, failed and empty, ROR looked up and named, durations), and the archive coverage and threshold.
  2. **Annotations** for warnings: coverage below 95%, a newest day older than 3 weekdays, pending counts rising.
  3. **Failure issue**: a `notify` job, on `failure()` of a scheduled run, opens or updates one issue labelled `pipeline-failure` with the run URL (`issues: write` only there).
  4. Add `%(asctime)s` to the plain log format.
  5. Optionally, a post-deploy smoke check: `curl` the deployed `index.json` and compare `generated_at`.
- **Benefit.** A single maintainer sees the state of the pipeline on one page and hears about failures within a day.
- **Risks and trade-offs.** Issue noise during outages, so deduplicate on the label.
- **Dependencies.** REL-1, CIT-4 and DATA-3 supply the signals.
- **Verification.** A tie (1 vote for P1, 1 for P2; one review failed), so it stays **P1**.

### 17.2 Worth exploring later

- **Trending topics.** Title n-gram frequencies per week against a 12-week baseline, computed at build. This is only meaningful once 12 months of day files exist.
- **Structured extraction with an LLM, in bounded form.** The target is facts the abstract lacks and a filter would use: datasets and benchmarks used, code release. Validate precision on a labelled sample first.
- **A weekly digest for the maintainer only.** A step in `update.yml` e-mails the `feeds.json` matches to one secret address.
- **Multi-category support** (ARCH-4): a merged view of cs.CV and cs.LG with cross-list dedup.

### 17.3 Not worth building (and why)

- **Accounts, sync or per-user e-mail digests.** They need a server and personal data. Export and import plus feeds cover the need.
- **Per-paper LLM summaries.** The abstract already is a summary (192 words in median). It would cost API spend and be sometimes wrong. This is "AI for the sake of AI" here.
- **Embeddings, semantic search or topic clusters** before lexical related papers prove insufficient. That is about 6 MB of vectors a month to ship, plus a model in CI.
- **A stored citation graph.** Recent papers have almost no citers (3,404 of 3,654 counted recent papers have 0). Link out to Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar instead.
- **26k static per-paper pages** just so the Zotero connector detects items. RIS and BibTeX import cover it at a fraction of the cost.
- **A WebGL graph renderer.** Drawing is the smaller half of the cost (33 ms of 130 ms per frame). The Worker and smaller defaults attack the larger half.
- **Lexical "related papers".** Semantic Scholar, Connected Papers and alphaXiv already do it from an arXiv id. Link out instead (17.1 #5).
- **Comments, upvotes, community curation, chat with a paper, recommendations trained on ratings.** alphaXiv, Hugging Face Daily Papers, Scholar Inbox and Semantic Scholar's feeds do these, with accounts. Link out instead.

### 17.4 Positioning

A completeness review proposed a statement for the README and the About page (SEC-9) that promises only what the code does:

1. Preprint Radar: every cs.CV paper arXiv announced in recent weeks, with the labs that wrote it.
2. Filter the daily list by institution, author, accepted venue and words, which arXiv's own listing cannot do.
3. See who works on what: rankings and a map of institutions and authors, by papers or by citations.
4. Browse the most cited cs.CV papers of each year since 2010, narrowed to a lab, an author or a topic.
5. No account and no tracking: your reading list and settings stay in this browser.

Before claiming that no other free tool filters the daily list by institution, check alphaXiv's and Hugging Face's organisation features. It is a 15-minute check that could not be done offline.

## 19. Recommended roadmap

Each phase ships on its own and leaves the site better than before. Durations assume one person working with an AI coding assistant, as the project has been built so far.

### Phase 0: Stop publishing wrong data (1–2 days)

**Goal:** the data on the site is correct, and every future mistake is caught.

1. DATA-2: reset the mapping on author-list changes, enforce the invariants, and repair the 2 records.
2. AFF-1 and AFF-2: accent- and punctuation-folded name filter, name lists, IEEE grades, obfuscated e-mails.
3. AFF-5 and AFF-6: junk labels, "is with" sentences, "also with".
4. DATA-1, layer 1: `reclean`, run once. It fixes the 36 rejected strings, the 107 last-author shapes and the new rules above in stored data.
5. DATA-3: `validate`, with structural rules failing and heuristic rules warning, run before *Commit data* and in CI.
6. CIT-1, CIT-2, CIT-3 and CIT-11: the archive failure modes, and selection decoupled from pruning (needed before any backfill).
7. AFF-15: the length guard in the graph.
8. TEST-1 (`fail_under = 100`), DATA-6 (index written early), DATA-7 (`.tmp`).
9. SEC-9 and SEC-10: an About section with a correction contact and a privacy note.

*Exit criteria:* `validate` passes with zero structural violations. `stats --affiliations` exists and is recorded as a baseline.

### Phase 1: Make the pipeline self-healing and observable (about 1 week)

**Goal:** failures are loud, cheap and recoverable, and heuristic fixes reach stored data automatically.

1. REL-1 (drift breaker plus canary), REL-2 (circuit breaker plus deadline), CI-1 (robust commit), CI-2 (summary plus failure issue), CI-3 (deploy gating), CIT-4 (S2 key fail-fast, staleness).
2. DATA-1, layers 2 and 3: `EXTRACTOR_VERSION`, `reextract` with a small daily budget, and the front-matter cache (location decided, §21).
3. AFF-Q: the quality section in `stats`. TEST-4 and TEST-5: negative tests and golden fixtures.
4. DOC-1: the runbook. REL-3: the license and data terms.

*Exit criteria:* a simulated arXiv outage, layout change, S2 403 and rebase conflict each end in a clear red run or a summary warning, and nothing is lost.

### Phase 2: Search precision and the daily workflow (1–2 weeks)

**Goal:** the core persona can triage a day in minutes and trust every filter.

1. SRCH-1 to SRCH-5: exact clicked filters, phrase and exclusion scope and tokenization, prefix and fuzzy tuning, history, archive permalinks. Then SRCH-17 (acronym filters), with TEST-16 pinning relevance.
2. PROD-2 to PROD-5: keyboard triage, seen, since last visit; saved searches and watchlist; reading-list import and safety; BibTeX correctness.
3. UX-4 first (focus hidden by the sticky bar), then UX-1, UX-3 and UX-5 to UX-12: the accessibility batch, mostly CSS and ARIA.
4. PROD-6 to PROD-10: name the category, about page, citation transparency, result export, recent-count noise.
5. TEST-7 and TEST-8: web coverage and a Playwright smoke test.

*Exit criteria:* the substring precision probes ("Hao Li", "Intel") return exact sets, and `"zero shot"` finds the zero-shot papers. Back works. The smoke test is green in CI.

### Phase 3: Scale and richer metadata (2–4 weeks, gated by decisions)

**Goal:** the product covers a continuous year, with DOIs, venues and institution ids.

1. PERF-1: bounded periods, async index build, then per-month search shards.
2. META-1 spike, then S2 sidecar (DOI, published venue, author ids). AFF-14: ROR ids. AFF-3: the OpenAlex retry policy (after its spike).
3. PROD-1: fill the 11-month gap (backfill or catalog route, per §21), once CIT-11 has landed. Then decide retention (ARCH-1): after CIT-11, pruning is only a storage decision.
4. PERF-2 and PERF-3: graph defaults and the clique cap. UX-2: graph touch and keyboard.
5. META-2 to META-4: weekly refresh from the catalog, provenance, the venue table.
6. PERF-11 and PERF-12: a lazy index build and split citation counts, which cut the landing cost.

*Exit criteria:* a year of day files loads without freezing a phone. Venue coverage of the archive rises above 52%.

### Phase 4: Differentiating features and long-term architecture (ongoing)

- Card link-outs, institution and venue feeds, rising papers, graph ego networks and export (§17.1).
- AFF-13 / ARCH-3 (canonicalization in the pipeline), AFF-H (author identity), ARCH-2 (enrichment step interface), DEBT-2 and DEBT-3 (view model, GraphView split), the PERF-2 Worker.
- Multi-category support (ARCH-4), if wanted.
